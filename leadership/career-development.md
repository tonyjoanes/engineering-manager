# Career Development

Guiding your team members' growth and advancement.

## Why Career Development Matters

**For the individual:**
- Skill growth and learning
- Career advancement
- Increased engagement
- Job satisfaction
- Marketability

**For you and the team:**
- Retention (main reason people leave)
- Higher performance
- Succession planning
- Recruiting advantage
- Team capability scaling

**The reality:** If you don't help them grow, they'll leave to grow elsewhere.

## The Manager's Role

### What You Should Do

✓ **Understand their goals** - Where do they want to go?
✓ **Provide opportunities** - Give them challenging work
✓ **Give feedback** - Help them see blind spots
✓ **Advocate for them** - Push for promotion when ready
✓ **Remove obstacles** - Clear path for their growth
✓ **Connect them** - Introduce to people and opportunities

### What You Should NOT Do

✗ **Assume one path** - Not everyone wants management
✗ **Hold them back** - Fearing you'll lose them
✗ **Make empty promises** - Can't guarantee promotion timeline
✗ **Clone yourself** - They don't need to be like you
✗ **Ignore their interests** - Growth on their terms, not just yours

## Understanding Career Goals

### The Career Development Conversation

**When:** At least quarterly, separate from 1:1s

**Structure (60 min):**

**1. Their Vision (15 min)**
- "Where do you see yourself in 2-3 years?"
- "What kind of work excites you?"
- "What would you like to be known for?"
- "What impact do you want to have?"

**2. Current State (15 min)**
- "What are you good at?"
- "What do you want to get better at?"
- "What energizes you? What drains you?"
- "What opportunities are you looking for?"

**3. Gap Analysis (15 min)**
- What skills need development?
- What experiences are missing?
- What's blocking progress?
- What support is needed?

**4. Action Plan (15 min)**
- Specific development goals
- Projects or assignments
- Learning resources
- Timeline and milestones
- How you'll support

### Different Career Paths

**Management Track:**
- Leading people
- Organizational impact
- Strategy and planning
- Growing others

**Individual Contributor (IC) Track:**
- Technical depth
- Expert in domain
- Architecture and technical strategy
- Leading through expertise

**Hybrid Roles:**
- Tech lead (IC with leadership)
- Staff+ engineer (influence without reports)
- Technical program manager

**Important:** Neither path is "better" - they're different

### Red Flags in Career Conversations

**"I want to be promoted in 6 months"**
- Unrealistic timeline expectations
- **Address:** Explain actual timeline and what's required

**"I just want stability"**
- Might be burned out
- **Probe:** What's driving this? Temporary or permanent?

**"I have no idea"**
- Lack of direction
- **Help:** Explore options, try different things

**"I want your job"**
- Could be good (ambitious) or bad (undermining)
- **Clarify:** Why? What about that role appeals?

## Creating Development Plans

### The Development Plan Template

```markdown
# Development Plan - [Name] - [Date]

## Career Goal (1-3 years)
[Where they want to be]

## Current Strengths
- [Strength 1]
- [Strength 2]
- [Strength 3]

## Development Areas
- [Skill/area to develop 1]
- [Skill/area to develop 2]
- [Skill/area to develop 3]

## Action Plan

### Goal 1: [Specific skill/area]
**Why:** [Impact and motivation]
**How:**
- [Action item 1 with timeline]
- [Action item 2 with timeline]
**Success looks like:** [Measurable outcome]
**Support needed:** [From manager]

### Goal 2: [Specific skill/area]
[Same structure]

### Goal 3: [Specific skill/area]
[Same structure]

## Check-in Schedule
- Monthly: Quick progress check
- Quarterly: Full review and adjust plan
```

### SMART Development Goals

**Specific:** Not "get better at coding" but "learn Rust and build a service"
**Measurable:** How will you know they achieved it?
**Achievable:** Stretch but realistic
**Relevant:** Aligns with their goals and role
**Time-bound:** Clear timeline (quarter, 6 months, year)

**Examples:**

❌ "Improve leadership skills"
✓ "Lead the Q2 planning process and get feedback from 5 team members"

❌ "Learn new technology"
✓ "Complete distributed systems course and design an event-driven system by Q3"

❌ "Be more senior"
✓ "Mentor 2 junior engineers and get promoted to Senior in next review cycle"

## Development Opportunities

### Stretch Assignments

**Characteristics:**
- 10-20% beyond current capability
- Has support available
- Real business value
- Allows learning from doing

**Examples:**
- Lead a project (for first-time lead)
- Present to leadership (for visibility)
- Own a system (for ownership)
- Mentor someone (for management track)
- Design an architecture (for senior growth)

### Skill Development Activities

**70-20-10 Rule:**
- 70% from challenging assignments
- 20% from relationships and feedback
- 10% from formal training

**On-the-job:**
- Leading projects
- Cross-team collaboration
- Incident response
- Architecture design
- Code reviews (giving and receiving)

**Learning from others:**
- Mentorship (giving and receiving)
- Shadowing more senior people
- Pair programming
- Tech talks
- Book clubs

**Formal learning:**
- Online courses (Coursera, Udemy, etc.)
- Conferences
- Workshops
- Certifications (if valuable)
- Reading (books, papers)

### Visibility Opportunities

**Why it matters:** Can't get promoted if no one knows your work

**How to create visibility:**
- Present at team meetings
- Write architecture docs
- Give tech talks
- Blog posts (internal or external)
- Represent team in cross-org meetings
- Lead initiatives that touch other teams

## Leveling and Promotion

### Understanding Your Levels

**Most companies have tiers like:**
- Junior Engineer (L1-2)
- Engineer (L3)
- Senior Engineer (L4-5)
- Staff Engineer (L6)
- Principal/Distinguished (L7+)

**Each level has expectations around:**
- Scope of impact
- Technical complexity
- Independence
- Leadership/influence

### Promotion Readiness

**Key question:** Are they already performing at next level?

**They're ready when:**
- Consistently operating at next level for 6+ months
- Have demonstrated key skills of next level
- Team and stakeholders see them at that level
- Clear evidence and examples
- Company has budget and need

**They're NOT ready when:**
- Just hit current level expectations
- Inconsistent performance
- Missing key skills
- Only you see it (not rest of org)
- Recency bias (one great project)

### The Promotion Process

**Your job as manager:**

**1. Set clear expectations**
- What does next level look like?
- Specific behaviors and outcomes
- Timeline (typically 1-2 years between levels)

**2. Create opportunities**
- Give them next-level work
- Ensure visibility
- Connect them with stakeholders

**3. Track evidence**
- Document achievements
- Collect feedback from others
- Build the promotion case

**4. Advocate**
- Write strong promotion packet
- Present to calibration committee
- Fight for them if needed

**5. Manage expectations**
- Be honest about timeline
- Explain obstacles
- Don't make promises you can't keep

### The Promotion Conversation

**When they ask "When will I be promoted?"**

**Good response:**
"Let's talk about what promotion to [level] looks like. Here are the expectations: [specifics]. Here's where you are now: [assessment]. Here's the gap: [specific areas]. And here's how we can work on closing that gap: [plan].

Typically it takes [realistic timeline] to make this jump, but it depends on your growth and business needs. Let's check in quarterly on progress."

**Bad response:**
"Soon" (vague)
"When you're ready" (circular)
"It's not up to me" (avoiding responsibility)

### When to Say No

**If they're not ready:**

Be honest and specific:
"I don't think you're ready for promotion to [level] yet. Here's specifically why: [clear gaps with examples]. Here's what would need to change: [specific improvements]. Let's create a plan to get you there."

**If there's no path:**

Be transparent:
"Honestly, we don't have headcount for another [level] right now. You're doing great work, but there's not a path to promotion in the next year. I want to be transparent so you can make informed decisions about your career."

## Managing Different Scenarios

### The High Performer

**Characteristics:**
- Consistently exceeds expectations
- Takes on challenges
- Helps others
- Looking for growth

**Your approach:**
- Give them hardest problems
- Increase scope and impact
- Fast-track promotion when ready
- Don't burn them out
- Keep them challenged

**Risk:** They'll leave if not growing

### The Solid Performer

**Characteristics:**
- Meets expectations consistently
- Reliable and steady
- May not seek growth actively
- Content with current level

**Your approach:**
- Recognize their value
- Offer growth if interested
- Don't force management track
- Provide variety and challenge
- Appreciate their stability

**Risk:** Neglecting them for high performers

### The Promotion Seeker

**Characteristics:**
- Focused on title and level
- May lack patience
- Cares about status

**Your approach:**
- Redirect to impact focus
- Set clear expectations
- Provide honest feedback
- Show path but not shortcuts
- Manage timeline expectations

**Red flag:** Only motivated by title, not growth

### The Career Changer

**Characteristics:**
- Wants to switch from IC to management (or reverse)
- Trying new direction
- May take step back

**Your approach:**
- Explore motivations deeply
- Provide trial opportunities
- Be honest about trade-offs
- Support the transition
- Allow experimentation

**Key question:** "What about [new path] appeals to you that you're not getting now?"

### The Plateaued Engineer

**Characteristics:**
- Been at same level for years
- Not interested in promotion
- Still performing adequately
- Happy where they are

**Your approach:**
- Respect their choice
- Ensure quality stays high
- Provide variety
- Don't pressure for growth
- Value their stability

**Important:** Not everyone needs to advance - that's okay

## Career Coaching Techniques

### The GROW Model

See [GROW Model](./grow-model.md) for full framework.

**For career discussions:**
- **Goal:** Where do you want to be?
- **Reality:** Where are you now?
- **Options:** What paths could get you there?
- **Will:** What will you actually do?

### Powerful Questions

**Discovery:**
- "What would make this your dream job?"
- "What are you naturally good at?"
- "What do you do when you lose track of time?"
- "What would you regret not learning?"

**Challenge:**
- "What's holding you back?"
- "What would you do if you weren't afraid?"
- "What would need to be true for you to...?"
- "What's the smallest step you could take?"

**Reflection:**
- "What have you learned about yourself?"
- "What patterns do you notice?"
- "What feedback have you gotten consistently?"
- "What assumptions might you be making?"

## Common Career Development Mistakes

### ❌ Assuming Everyone Wants Management
- Not everyone does
- **Fix:** Ask, don't assume

### ❌ Holding People Back
- Fearing loss of top performer
- **Fix:** Help them grow or they'll leave

### ❌ Promoting Too Early
- Setting people up to fail
- **Fix:** Ensure they're ready

### ❌ Vague Feedback
- "You need more executive presence"
- **Fix:** Specific, actionable guidance

### ❌ No Follow-Through
- Make plan, never revisit
- **Fix:** Regular check-ins on development

### ❌ One-Size-Fits-All
- Same advice for everyone
- **Fix:** Personalized development

## Succession Planning

### Why It Matters
- What if you leave?
- What if star performer leaves?
- How do you scale?

### Identify High Potentials

**Who:**
- High performers + high potential
- Ambitious and curious
- Leadership qualities
- Coachable

**Invest in them:**
- More mentorship time
- Stretch assignments
- Visibility opportunities
- Honest feedback

**Risk:** Don't neglect others

### Preparing Your Replacement

**If you want to be promoted, someone needs to take your job:**

- Delegate key responsibilities
- Give them visibility with your manager
- Let them lead in your absence
- Share your knowledge and context
- Advocate for their promotion

## External Growth

### When to Support Leaving

**Sometimes the best growth is elsewhere:**
- No path to their goal in your org
- Opportunity too good to pass up
- They've outgrown the role
- Different industry interests

**How to handle:**
- Support them gracefully
- Offer to be reference
- Help them transition
- Stay in touch (boomerangs are valuable)
- Don't burn bridges

**Your reputation:**
- Alumni speak about you
- Being supportive attracts talent
- "Career accelerator" reputation

## Tracking Development

### Quarterly Development Check-ins

**Agenda:**
1. Review last quarter's goals
2. Assess progress
3. Adjust plan if needed
4. Set next quarter's goals
5. Identify support needed

**Questions:**
- What progress did you make?
- What was harder than expected?
- What do you want to focus on next?
- What support do you need from me?

### Annual Career Review

**Separate from performance review:**

**Reflect:**
- How did you grow this year?
- What are you proud of?
- What surprised you?

**Plan:**
- Where do you want to be next year?
- What skills do you want to develop?
- What opportunities interest you?

**Align:**
- How does this fit with team needs?
- What can I do to support?
- What might get in the way?
