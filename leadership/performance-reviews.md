# Performance Reviews

A comprehensive guide to conducting fair, effective performance evaluations.

## Purpose of Performance Reviews

**For the Employee:**
- Understand how they're performing
- Get recognition for achievements
- Identify areas for growth
- Set goals for next period
- Understand compensation decisions

**For the Manager:**
- Document performance
- Provide structured feedback
- Make promotion/compensation decisions
- Set expectations
- Track improvement or decline

**For the Company:**
- Calibrate across teams
- Make fair compensation decisions
- Identify high performers and low performers
- Legal documentation (if termination needed)

**The review should have NO surprises** - Everything should have been discussed already.

## Performance Review Cycle

### Typical Timeline

**Annual cycle example:**
- **Month 1 (Jan):** Set goals for the year
- **Month 6 (June):** Mid-year check-in
- **Month 11 (Nov):** Self-review and peer feedback
- **Month 12 (Dec):** Manager writes review
- **Month 12 (Dec):** Calibration meetings
- **Month 1 (Jan):** Deliver reviews and compensation
- **Repeat**

### The Review Process

**1. Self-Review (Employee)**
- Reflect on achievements
- Assess against goals
- Identify growth areas
- Request specific feedback

**2. Peer Feedback (Collected by Manager)**
- 3-5 peers provide input
- Structured questions
- Anonymous or attributed (varies by company)

**3. Manager writes Review**
- Synthesize feedback
- Add own observations
- Rate performance
- Draft next period goals

**4. Calibration (Managers)**
- Compare ratings across teams
- Ensure consistency
- Adjust if needed

**5. Delivery Conversation**
- Manager presents review
- Discussion and questions
- Agree on goals
- Discuss compensation

## Writing the Review

### Review Structure

**Common sections:**
1. **Summary/Overview** - High-level assessment
2. **Key Accomplishments** - Major wins
3. **Goals Achievement** - Progress on previous goals
4. **Core Competencies** - Technical, collaboration, etc.
5. **Areas for Development** - Growth opportunities
6. **Next Period Goals** - What's ahead
7. **Overall Rating** - Performance level

### Rating Scales

**Common scale (5-point):**
- **5 - Exceptional:** Far exceeds expectations, top 5% of company
- **4 - Exceeds:** Consistently goes above and beyond
- **3 - Meets:** Solid performance, meeting all expectations
- **2 - Partially Meets:** Some gaps, needs improvement
- **1 - Does Not Meet:** Significantly below expectations

**Important:** 3 is GOOD, not average. Most people should be 3s.

### Writing Guidelines

**Do:**
✓ Use specific examples with dates and impact
✓ Focus on observable behaviors and outcomes
✓ Balance positive and constructive
✓ Connect to company values
✓ Be honest but kind
✓ Cite peer feedback where relevant

**Don't:**
✗ Use vague generalities
✗ Make it personal or about personality
✗ Save up feedback for the review
✗ Compare to other team members
✗ Let recency bias dominate (recent work only)
✗ Use jargon or corporate speak

### Example: Good vs Bad

**Bad (vague):**
"Alex is a strong contributor who sometimes needs to work on communication. Overall good work this year."

**Good (specific):**
"Alex delivered three major features this year: the payment refactoring (Q1), the API migration (Q2), and the mobile SDK (Q4). The payment refactoring was particularly impressive, reducing transaction time by 40% and eliminating a class of bugs that had caused 15% of our customer support tickets.

In terms of communication, Alex has grown significantly. Early in the year, design documents often lacked context, which led to confusion in reviews. After feedback, Alex started including 'why' sections and diagrams, which has made reviews much more efficient. Recent example: the mobile SDK design doc (Oct) was comprehensive and well-received by all reviewers.

One area for continued development: proactive status updates. On the API migration project, there were delays that weren't communicated until standup when others were already blocked. Moving forward, I'd like to see Alex send updates when blockers emerge rather than waiting for the next sync."

**Why it's better:**
- Specific projects with quarters
- Quantified impact (40%, 15%)
- Shows growth over time
- Concrete example of issue
- Clear path forward

## Assessing Performance

### What to Evaluate

**Technical/Functional Skills:**
- Quality of work
- Technical complexity handled
- Problem-solving ability
- Domain expertise
- Learning and adaptation

**Delivery & Execution:**
- Meeting deadlines
- Project ownership
- Breaking down ambiguity
- Follow-through
- Managing dependencies

**Collaboration:**
- Teamwork
- Communication (written and verbal)
- Helping others
- Conflict resolution
- Cross-functional work

**Leadership (Level-Appropriate):**
- Mentoring
- Initiative
- Influence
- Technical leadership
- Decision-making

**Company Values:**
- How well they embody cultural values
- Team citizenship
- Customer focus
- Innovation

### Gathering Evidence

**Throughout the year, track:**
- Major projects and outcomes
- 1:1 notes and feedback given
- Code review comments (patterns)
- Peer feedback (informal)
- Incidents handled
- Presentations given
- Mentoring provided
- Initiative taken

**Keep a "wins" doc:**
- Monthly bullet points
- Makes review writing easier
- Prevents recency bias
- Great for promotion packets

### The "Data + Story" Method

**Structure your assessment as:**

```
[Behavior/Skill Area]

Data:
- Delivered 8 features vs team average of 6
- 95% of PRs approved within 24 hours
- Led onboarding of 2 new team members

Story:
[Name] has become the go-to person for complex API work. When the team faced the authentication refactor (our most complex project this year), [Name] volunteered to lead it despite the ambiguity. They broke it into phases, involved security early, and delivered on time with zero production issues. This demonstrated both technical strength and project leadership growth.

Impact:
Customers now experience 3x faster login, and our security posture improved significantly.
```

## Delivering the Review

### Before the Meeting

**Prepare:**
- Read your review aloud (catch awkward phrasing)
- Anticipate their reactions
- Review their self-assessment
- Have compensation info ready
- Block enough time (60-90 min)
- Private space, no interruptions

**Your mindset:**
- You're helping them succeed
- This is a gift, not punishment
- You care about their growth
- Be prepared for emotions

### The Conversation Structure (60-90 min)

**Opening (5 min)**
"Thanks for meeting. I want to share your performance review for this period. I'll walk through it, then we can discuss. Feel free to ask questions or share reactions along the way."

**Review Overview (10 min)**
- Start with overall rating and summary
- "Your rating is [X]. Here's what that means..."
- State your overall assessment clearly

**Walk Through Sections (30 min)**
- Accomplishments and strengths first
- Then development areas
- Use specific examples
- Allow reactions and questions
- Show peer feedback highlights

**Their Reactions (10 min)**
- "What's your reaction to this?"
- "Does this align with how you see your performance?"
- "What surprised you?"
- Listen without defending

**Forward-Looking (20 min)**
- Goals for next period
- Development plan
- How you'll support them
- Career aspirations
- Compensation (if ready to discuss)

**Close (5 min)**
- Summarize key points
- Action items
- When to check in again
- Thank them for their work

### Handling Different Reactions

**Agreement/Acceptance:**
"Thank you for the feedback. This aligns with what I expected."
→ Great! Discuss goals and move forward.

**Surprise:**
"I didn't realize I was struggling with X."
→ "I should have been clearer in real-time. Let's make sure we're aligned going forward."

**Disagreement:**
"I don't think this is accurate."
→ "Help me understand your perspective. Can you give me examples of...?"
Listen, acknowledge their view, but stand by your assessment if warranted.

**Disappointment:**
"I thought I was doing better than this."
→ "I understand. Let's talk about specific areas where there's a gap and how we can close it."

**Upset/Crying:**
→ Pause, offer tissue, give them space. "Take your time. This is hard to hear."

**Anger:**
→ Stay calm. "I can see you're frustrated. Do you need a break?" If escalating, reschedule.

### Difficult Review Scenarios

**Scenario: Low Performance Rating**

"I'm giving you a 2 rating, which means you're partially meeting expectations. I know this is disappointing. Let me be specific about the gaps:

[Specific issues with examples]

This isn't about your potential - it's about current performance. Here's what needs to change:

[Clear expectations]

I want to support you in improving. We'll check in weekly and revisit in 90 days."

**Scenario: No Promotion Despite Expectations**

"I know you were hoping for promotion this cycle. I want to be honest: you're not ready yet. Here's specifically why:

[Gap to next level with examples]

You're doing well at your current level, but the bar for [next level] requires [specific examples]. Here's what we can work on:

[Development plan]

I do see a path for you, but realistically it's [timeline] if we make these improvements."

**Scenario: Mixed Feedback (Peer Says One Thing, You See Another)**

"I know the peer feedback mentioned concerns about communication. I've seen this sometimes too - like in the incident last month. But I've also seen you improve, like in the recent design review. It's not consistent yet. Let's talk about what's making communication hard sometimes."

## The Compensation Discussion

### When to Discuss

**Options:**
1. **Same meeting as review** - Common, but can overshadow feedback
2. **Separate meeting** - Allows focus on development first
3. **Depends on news** - Bad news might need separate conversation

### Discussing Raises

**Good news:**
"Based on your strong performance, you're getting a [X%] raise, bringing your salary to [amount]. This puts you at [position relative to band/market]."

**No raise:**
"Given your performance rating and company budget, there's no merit increase this period. This is directly tied to the performance concerns we discussed. If performance improves, we can revisit."

**Small raise:**
"You're getting a [X%] raise. I know you may have expected more. Here's the reality: [company budget situation / performance gaps / market positioning]. Here's what would lead to a larger increase next time: [specific improvements]."

### Discussing Promotions

**Approved:**
"I'm excited to share that you're being promoted to [level] effective [date]. Your new salary will be [amount]. This recognizes the work you've been doing, particularly [examples]. Congratulations - you earned this."

**Denied:**
"I know you were hoping for promotion. Unfortunately, it didn't get approved this cycle. Here's why: [specific gaps]. This doesn't mean it won't happen - here's the path: [clear plan with realistic timeline]."

### Discussing Bonuses

Be clear about:
- Formula (if applicable)
- Company performance component
- Individual performance component
- Why they got what they got

## Calibration Meetings

### Purpose

**Ensure fairness:**
- Compare ratings across team/org
- Prevent manager bias
- Create consistency
- Align on bar

**Forced curve?**
Some companies force distribution (e.g., only 20% can be 4s). Be transparent about this.

### The Process

**Managers present each person:**
- Name and role
- Proposed rating
- Key achievements
- Justification

**Group discusses:**
- Is rating justified?
- Consistent with others at that level?
- Compare similar roles

**Adjust if needed:**
- May raise or lower ratings
- May adjust compensation
- Document reasoning

### Handling Rating Changes

**If your rating was changed:**
"After calibration with other managers, your rating was adjusted from [X] to [Y]. Here's why: [honest explanation]. I know this is frustrating. The feedback and development areas remain the same."

**Be honest:**
- Don't blame "the process"
- Own the final rating
- Explain the why

## After the Review

### Follow-Up

**Within a week:**
- Send written copy (if not done in meeting)
- Confirm goals in writing
- Schedule first check-in on goals
- Answer any follow-up questions

**Ongoing:**
- Regular feedback on goal progress
- Monthly check-ins on development areas
- Don't save feedback for next review
- Track wins for next cycle

### Performance Improvement

**If below expectations:**
- Weekly check-ins
- Clear metrics for improvement
- 30/60/90 day milestones
- May lead to PIP if no improvement

See [Performance Improvement Plans](./performance-improvement-plans.md)

## Common Mistakes

### ❌ Surprises in the Review
- Feedback they never heard before
- **Fix:** Give feedback in real-time

### ❌ Recency Bias
- Only remembering last 2 months
- **Fix:** Track all year, review timeline

### ❌ Halo/Horn Effect
- One thing colors everything
- **Fix:** Evaluate each area independently

### ❌ Comparing to Others
- "Better than Alex, worse than Sam"
- **Fix:** Compare to expectations for role, not peers

### ❌ Being Too Nice
- Can't give constructive feedback
- **Fix:** Remember that clarity is kindness

### ❌ Vague Language
- "Could be more proactive"
- **Fix:** Specific examples and behaviors

### ❌ Long Review, No Discussion
- Manager talks for 60 minutes
- **Fix:** Make it a conversation

## Self-Review Guidance

### Help Your Team Write Good Self-Reviews

**Provide structure:**
```
1. What were your major accomplishments?
2. What goals did you meet/miss and why?
3. What did you learn?
4. What do you want to work on?
5. How can I better support you?
```

**Encourage them to:**
- Be specific with examples
- Quantify impact where possible
- Be honest about struggles
- Connect work to business goals
- Think beyond just code

**Remind them:**
- This is your chance to highlight wins
- I may not remember everything
- Help me help you

## Peer Feedback Collection

### Requesting Peer Feedback

**Who to ask:**
- People they work closely with
- Cross-functional partners
- Different levels (peers, more senior)
- Ask the employee who they want feedback from

**Questions to ask:**
```
1. What are [Name]'s greatest strengths?
2. What is one area where [Name] could improve?
3. Can you share a specific example of [Name]'s impact?
4. What's it like to work with [Name]?
5. Anything else I should know for their review?
```

**Keep it:**
- Focused (5 questions max)
- Specific (ask for examples)
- Balanced (positive and constructive)
- Confidential (decide if you'll share verbatim)

### Using Peer Feedback

**Do:**
✓ Look for patterns
✓ Weight based on knowledge of person
✓ Synthesize themes
✓ Share key quotes (with permission)

**Don't:**
✗ Cherry-pick to support your view
✗ Let one outlier drive the review
✗ Break confidentiality if promised
✗ Make it a popularity contest

## Performance Review Templates

### Template: Strong Performer

```
Overall Rating: 4 (Exceeds Expectations)

[Name] had an excellent year, delivering significant impact on [area]. They exceeded expectations in both technical execution and team leadership.

Key Accomplishments:
• [Project 1]: [Impact with metrics]
• [Project 2]: [Impact with metrics]
• [Leadership example]: [Specific situation and outcome]

Strengths:
• Technical Excellence: [Specific examples]
• Collaboration: [Peer feedback quote]
• Initiative: [Example of going beyond]

Areas for Continued Growth:
• [Area with specific suggestion]

Goals for Next Period:
• [Goal 1]
• [Goal 2]
• [Goal 3]

[Name] is operating at a high level and positioned well for continued growth. I look forward to seeing their continued impact.
```

### Template: Meets Expectations with Development Areas

```
Overall Rating: 3 (Meets Expectations)

[Name] delivered solid work this year, meeting the core expectations for their role. There are also some areas for development that we'll focus on together.

Key Accomplishments:
• [Major deliverable with outcome]
• [Contribution to team]
• [Growth shown]

Strengths:
• [Strength 1 with example]
• [Strength 2 with example]

Development Areas:
• [Area 1]: [Specific examples and impact]. Going forward: [Clear expectation]
• [Area 2]: [Specific examples]. We'll work together on: [Plan]

Goals for Next Period:
• [Goal addressing development area]
• [New stretch goal]
• [Skill development goal]

I'm committed to supporting [Name]'s growth in these areas through [specific support].
```
