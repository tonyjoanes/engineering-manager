# Dealing with Underperformance

A comprehensive guide to identifying, addressing, and resolving performance issues.

## What is Underperformance?

**Definition:** Consistent failure to meet the expectations of the role.

**Not underperformance:**
- One-time mistakes
- Temporary dip during personal crisis
- New to role (still learning)
- Unclear expectations
- Lack of resources

**Is underperformance:**
- Consistently missing deadlines
- Poor quality work over time
- Behavioral issues affecting team
- Not responding to feedback
- Gap between expectations and delivery

**Key word: Consistent pattern, not isolated incidents**

## Early Warning Signs

### Performance Red Flags

**Delivery issues:**
- Missing deadlines repeatedly
- Low output compared to peers
- Work requires excessive rework
- Avoiding challenging tasks
- Making same mistakes repeatedly

**Quality issues:**
- Bugs reaching production
- Poor code review feedback
- Shortcuts and technical debt
- Lack of attention to detail
- Not following best practices

**Collaboration issues:**
- Not asking for help when stuck
- Poor communication
- Conflicts with teammates
- Dismissive of feedback
- Not participating in team activities

**Engagement issues:**
- Disengaged in meetings
- Lack of initiative
- Negative attitude
- Frequent absences
- Minimal effort

**Growth issues:**
- Not learning from mistakes
- Resistant to feedback
- No skill development
- Blaming others consistently
- Defensive when questioned

### Trust Your Gut

**If you're noticing:**
- Checking their work more than others
- Anxiety about their deliverables
- Avoiding assigning them work
- Compensating for their gaps
- Team complaints

**Probably underperformance - investigate.**

## Root Cause Analysis

**Before taking action, understand WHY**

### The 5 Categories

**1. Can't Do It (Capability)**
- Lacks skills for the role
- Technical gap
- Wrong level hired
- Cognitive limitations

**Signs:**
- Tries hard but can't deliver
- Eager but ineffective
- Overwhelmed despite effort
- Asks for help but doesn't improve

**2. Won't Do It (Motivation)**
- Has skills but not using them
- Disengaged or checked out
- Low effort
- Attitude issues

**Signs:**
- Can do it when they want to
- Inconsistent performance
- Minimal effort visible
- Negative attitude

**3. Doesn't Know What to Do (Clarity)**
- Unclear expectations
- Poor goal setting
- Lack of context
- Ambiguous role

**Signs:**
- Working on wrong things
- Confused about priorities
- Asking for direction constantly
- Wasted effort

**4. Blocked (Environmental)**
- Lack of resources
- Poor tools
- Organizational dysfunction
- Dependencies blocking

**Signs:**
- Frustrated but trying
- Pointing to real obstacles
- Works well when obstacles removed
- Team-wide issue, not individual

**5. Personal Issues (Circumstantial)**
- Health problems
- Family crisis
- Mental health
- Life circumstances

**Signs:**
- Recent change in performance
- Usually good, now struggling
- Personal situation visible
- Temporary (hopefully)

### Diagnosis Framework

**Ask yourself:**

1. **Do they have the capability?**
   - Have they done this successfully before?
   - Do they have the required skills?
   - Is the role beyond their level?

2. **Do they have the motivation?**
   - Are they trying?
   - Do they care about the work?
   - Are they engaged?

3. **Do they know what's expected?**
   - Have I been clear?
   - Do they understand the bar?
   - Is success well-defined?

4. **Do they have what they need?**
   - Resources available?
   - Tools working?
   - Support provided?
   - Obstacles in their way?

5. **Is something going on?**
   - Life circumstances?
   - Health issues?
   - Team dynamics?

**Different root causes require different solutions.**

## Early Intervention

**Address issues early - easier to fix**

### The First Conversation

**Timing:** As soon as you notice a pattern (2-3 instances)

**Setting:** Private 1:1, calm environment

**Structure:**

**1. State the observation (5 min)**
"I've noticed [specific pattern]. Let me give you examples:
- [Example 1 with date]
- [Example 2 with date]
- [Example 3 with date]"

**Be specific, not vague:**
❌ "Your performance is lacking"
✓ "You've missed the last 3 sprint deadlines, and your last 2 PRs had significant bugs that reached production"

**2. Ask for their perspective (10 min)**
"Help me understand what's happening from your side."
"What's making this difficult?"
"Is there something I'm missing?"

**Listen without interrupting. Really listen.**

**3. Collaborate on solution (10 min)**
"What do you think needs to change?"
"How can I support you?"
"What would help?"

**If they don't have solutions, propose:**
"Here's what I think would help: [specific actions]. What do you think?"

**4. Set clear expectations (5 min)**
"Going forward, here's what I need to see:
- [Specific expectation 1]
- [Specific expectation 2]
- [Specific expectation 3]"

**5. Follow up plan (5 min)**
"Let's check in on this in [timeframe, usually 2 weeks]. I'll schedule a follow-up."

**6. Document (after meeting)**
- Send email summary
- Note in your records
- Track for follow-up

### Intervention by Root Cause

**If Can't (Capability):**
- Provide training
- Pairing with senior engineer
- Smaller, manageable tasks
- Consider role change
- May need to exit if gap too large

**If Won't (Motivation):**
- Understand what drives them
- Connect work to purpose
- Remove obstacles
- Clear consequences
- May need to performance manage out

**If Doesn't Know (Clarity):**
- Set clearer expectations
- More frequent check-ins
- Written goals
- Examples of good work
- Feedback loops

**If Blocked (Environmental):**
- Remove obstacles
- Provide resources
- Fix tools
- Change processes
- May need to escalate org issues

**If Personal Issues:**
- Offer support (EAP, leave, flexibility)
- Adjust workload temporarily
- Show compassion
- Set timeline for reassessment
- HR involvement if needed

## The Performance Conversation Framework

### SBI + Expectations + Support

**Situation:**
"In last week's sprint planning meeting..."

**Behavior:**
"...you committed to 5 tickets but only completed 1."

**Impact:**
"This meant the team couldn't meet our sprint goal, and 2 other engineers were blocked waiting for your work."

**Expectation:**
"Going forward, I need you to either:
1. Commit to realistic amounts of work, or
2. Communicate blockers immediately when they arise"

**Support:**
"How can I help you estimate better? Would pairing on planning help?"

**Follow-up:**
"Let's review your sprint commitments in our 1:1s this month and see how it's going."

## Escalating to Formal Performance Management

### When to Formalize

**Move to formal performance management when:**
- Multiple informal conversations haven't worked (3+)
- Pattern continues over 1-2 months
- Impact is significant
- They're not responding to feedback
- You need documentation trail

**Types of formal performance management:**
1. **Written Performance Warning** - Document concerns, expectations, timeline
2. **Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)** - Structured 30-90 day plan
3. **Final Written Warning** - Last chance before termination

See [Performance Improvement Plans](./performance-improvement-plans.md) for full PIP details.

### The Written Warning

**When:** After informal conversations haven't worked, before PIP

**What it is:**
- Formal documentation
- Sent via email (not just verbal)
- States problem, expectations, timeline
- Creates paper trail

**Template:**
```
Subject: Performance Concerns - [Name]

Dear [Name],

Following our conversations on [date], [date], and [date], I'm writing to formally document performance concerns and expectations.

CONCERNS:
1. [Specific issue with examples and dates]
2. [Specific issue with examples and dates]

EXPECTATIONS:
Going forward, I need to see:
1. [Specific, measurable expectation]
2. [Specific, measurable expectation]

TIMELINE:
We will review progress in our 1:1s over the next 4 weeks. By [date], I expect to see significant improvement in the areas outlined above.

SUPPORT:
I am providing:
- [Resource/support 1]
- [Resource/support 2]

Please acknowledge receipt of this email and let me know if you have questions.

[Your Name]
```

**Copy HR on this email**

### Decision Tree

```
Performance issue identified
         ↓
First conversation (informal)
         ↓
    Improved? → YES → Continue monitoring
         ↓ NO
Second conversation (informal)
         ↓
    Improved? → YES → Continue monitoring
         ↓ NO
Third conversation (informal)
         ↓
    Improved? → YES → Continue monitoring
         ↓ NO
Written Warning (formal)
         ↓
    Improved? → YES → Continue monitoring
         ↓ NO
Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)
         ↓
    Met PIP goals? → YES → Continue employment
         ↓ NO
Termination
```

**Timeline:** Typically 2-4 months from first conversation to termination

## Managing During the Process

### Frequent Check-ins

**During performance management:**
- Weekly 1:1s minimum
- Document every conversation
- Provide specific feedback
- Track improvements and issues
- Adjust support as needed

**Don't:**
- Ghost them between meetings
- Save up feedback
- Spring surprises
- Give up on them

**Balance:**
- Give them chance to improve
- Also protect the team
- Be fair but firm

### Providing Feedback

**Be specific and timely:**
❌ "You need to improve your code quality"
✓ "In PR #234, there were 5 bugs that reached production. Here's what I'd like to see: unit tests covering edge cases, error handling for null values, and code review before submitting."

**Focus on behaviors and outcomes:**
❌ "Your attitude is bad"
✓ "In the last 3 stand-ups, when teammates asked questions, you responded with sarcasm. I need professional, respectful communication."

**Acknowledge progress:**
"I've seen improvement in X. Keep that up. We still need to work on Y."

### Documentation

**Document everything:**
- Date and summary of every conversation
- Specific examples of underperformance
- Examples of improvement (or lack thereof)
- Support and resources provided
- Employee responses and commitments

**Why:**
- Legal protection
- Fair assessment
- Reference for decisions
- Prevents "he said, she said"

**Template:**
```
1:1 Notes - [Name] - [Date]

Performance Discussion:
- Discussed: [Topic]
- Examples provided: [Specifics]
- Employee response: [What they said]
- Agreed actions: [Commitments]
- Follow-up: [When and what]

Progress since last meeting:
✓ [Improvement]
✗ [Continued issue]

Next steps: [What's next]
```

## When Performance Improves

### Successful Turnaround

**Signs of real improvement:**
- Meeting expectations consistently (4-6 weeks)
- Proactive communication
- Quality up
- Team feedback positive
- Sustained, not temporary

**When you see improvement:**
- Acknowledge it explicitly
- Positive reinforcement
- Continue monitoring (don't assume it's permanent)
- Gradually reduce oversight
- Remove from formal process after sustained improvement

**Script:**
"I want to acknowledge the improvement I've seen over the last 6 weeks. Your deliveries have been on time, code quality is much better, and you're communicating proactively. This is exactly what I need to see. Let's continue this trajectory."

**Don't:**
- Assume it's permanent too quickly
- Stop feedback
- Forget what happened
- Relax too much

**Continue regular 1:1s and feedback for another 2-3 months**

## When Performance Doesn't Improve

### Making the Termination Decision

**Terminate when:**
- Multiple conversations haven't worked
- PIP not met
- Impact on team is significant
- No meaningful improvement
- You've given adequate time and support

**Consult:**
- Your manager
- HR
- Legal (if needed)

**Don't terminate for:**
- First offense
- Without documentation
- Without giving them a chance
- During protected leave
- In protected classes without clear performance documentation

### The Termination Meeting

**Who should be there:**
- You
- HR representative
- Their manager (you)
- Possibly your manager

**When:**
- Early in week (not Friday)
- Morning
- Private location
- 15-30 minutes

**Script:**
"I need to let you know that we're ending your employment effective today. Despite our conversations on [dates], and the performance improvement plan from [date] to [date], you haven't met the expectations we outlined. This decision is final.

[HR person] will walk you through the logistics: final paycheck, benefits, returning equipment.

We'll escort you to collect your personal items. Do you have any questions?"

**Stay calm and brief:**
- Don't argue or debate
- Don't get emotional
- Don't apologize for the decision
- Be professional and respectful
- Follow HR/legal process exactly

**Logistics:**
- IT access removed immediately
- Equipment collected
- Final paycheck (check local laws)
- COBRA information
- Escort out

See [Difficult Conversations](./difficult-conversations.md) for more details.

### After Termination

**Team communication:**
```
"I want to let you know that [Name] is no longer with the company as of [date]. I can't discuss the details, but I want to address the transition.

Here's how we'll handle their work:
- [Assignment 1]
- [Assignment 2]

Any questions about the team or our work?"
```

**Don't:**
- Bad-mouth them
- Share details
- Discuss the decision
- Let team speculate endlessly

**Do:**
- Be professional
- Focus on transition
- Answer team concerns about work
- Move forward

## Alternatives to Termination

### Sometimes firing isn't the answer

**Consider:**

**1. Role Change**
- Move to different team
- Different responsibilities
- Better fit for skills
- Fresh start

**When:** Skills don't match role, but valuable elsewhere

**2. Managed Transition**
- Give them 30-90 days to find new job
- Provide references
- Severance package
- Exit with dignity

**When:** Not working out, but they're trying and decent person

**3. Leave of Absence**
- Medical leave
- Personal leave
- Mental health break
- Return with fresh start

**When:** Personal circumstances causing issues, likely temporary

**4. Reduced Responsibilities**
- Scope down role
- Lower expectations
- Acceptance they won't advance
- Steady state

**When:** Good enough but won't grow, and that's okay

**5. Coaching/Support Intensive**
- Executive coach
- Therapy/EAP
- Extensive mentoring
- Second chance with support

**When:** High potential, temporary struggle, worth the investment

## The Emotional Toll

### On You as Manager

**This is hard:**
- Feel like you're failing them
- Stress and anxiety
- Second-guessing yourself
- Impact on your sleep
- Dreading conversations

**Normal to feel:**
- Guilty
- Anxious
- Frustrated
- Relieved (when resolved)
- Sad

**Take care of yourself:**
- Debrief with your manager
- Talk to peer managers
- Therapy/coaching
- Boundaries (don't ruminate 24/7)
- Remember: you gave them chances

**Remember:**
- You didn't create their performance issues
- You gave them opportunities
- You have responsibility to team
- Clear is kind
- Not helping them to avoid hard conversations

### On the Team

**Team is watching:**
- How you handle this
- Whether you address issues
- Fairness of process
- Professionalism

**Team needs:**
- To see you address underperformance
- Confidence standards are maintained
- Fair treatment
- Closure after departure

**Don't let underperformance fester:**
- Damages team morale
- Others compensate
- Resentment builds
- Lowers bar

## Prevention

### Hire Right

**Prevent underperformance by hiring well:**
- Clear expectations in job description
- Thorough interview process
- Skills assessment
- Reference checks
- Bar raising

See [Hiring](./hiring.md)

### Onboard Well

**Set up for success:**
- Clear expectations from day one
- Proper training
- Mentorship
- Early feedback
- 30-60-90 day check-ins

See [Onboarding](./onboarding.md)

### Continuous Feedback

**Don't wait for reviews:**
- Regular 1:1s
- Immediate feedback
- Clear expectations
- Course corrections early
- No surprises

See [Feedback Models](./feedback-models.md)

### Clear Standards

**Make expectations visible:**
- Role descriptions
- Career ladders
- Code review standards
- Documented processes
- Examples of excellence

**When everyone knows the bar, easier to meet it.**

## Legal Considerations

### Protect Yourself and Company

**Document everything:**
- Conversations
- Examples
- Support provided
- Timeline

**Consult:**
- HR on all formal actions
- Legal if complex situation
- Follow company process exactly

**Watch for:**
- Protected classes (age, race, gender, disability, etc.)
- Recent complaints of harassment/discrimination
- Retaliation claims
- Timing around pregnancy, medical leave, etc.

**Not saying don't fire protected classes:**
- Just document extra carefully
- Ensure performance issues are real
- Treat same as anyone else
- Have strong paper trail

**When in doubt, consult legal**

## Common Mistakes

### ❌ Avoiding the Conversation

**Problem:** Hope it goes away, get worse

**Fix:** Address early and directly

### ❌ Unclear Expectations

**Problem:** They don't know what you want

**Fix:** Be specific and measurable

### ❌ No Documentation

**Problem:** Can't remember, can't defend decision

**Fix:** Write everything down

### ❌ Too Much Time

**Problem:** Letting it drag on for years

**Fix:** 2-4 months max from first conversation to decision

### ❌ Inconsistent Standards

**Problem:** Hold some people to standards, not others

**Fix:** Apply same bar to everyone

### ❌ Surprise Termination

**Problem:** Fire without warning or chances

**Fix:** Progressive discipline, clear communication

### ❌ Personal Feelings

**Problem:** Like/dislike affects judgment

**Fix:** Focus on performance, not personality

## When You're Wrong

### Reconsidering Your Assessment

**Maybe it's not underperformance if:**
- Your expectations were unclear
- You didn't provide necessary resources
- Everyone on team struggling (systematic issue)
- Recent major life event (temporary)
- Your bias affecting judgment

**If you realize you're wrong:**
- Acknowledge it
- Apologize
- Reset expectations
- Provide support
- Learn from it

**It's okay to change your mind based on new information**
