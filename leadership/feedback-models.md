# Feedback Models

Frameworks for delivering effective, actionable feedback that drives improvement and builds trust.

## Why Feedback Matters

- **Growth catalyst** - People can't improve what they don't know about
- **Course correction** - Catch issues early before they become problems
- **Recognition** - Positive feedback reinforces good behaviors
- **Trust building** - Regular feedback creates psychological safety
- **Performance management** - Documentation for reviews and PIPs

## Core Principles

1. **Timely** - Give feedback soon after the event (within 24-48 hours)
2. **Specific** - Use concrete examples, not generalities
3. **Actionable** - Focus on what they can change
4. **Balanced** - Mix positive and constructive
5. **Regular** - Make it a habit, not an event
6. **Two-way** - Ask for their perspective

## Radical Candor Framework

### The 2x2 Matrix

```
                    Challenge Directly
                            ↑
                            |
        Obnoxious    |  Radical Candor
        Aggression   |  ★ The Goal
    ←───────────────┼───────────────→
        Manipulative |  Ruinous
        Insincerity  |  Empathy
                     |
                     ↓
                Care Personally
```

### Four Quadrants

**Radical Candor (Care + Challenge)** ★
- You care about the person AND give direct feedback
- "I'm telling you this because I care about your growth"
- Example: "Your presentation had great data, but you lost the audience with jargon. Let's work on simplifying your message."

**Ruinous Empathy (Care, No Challenge)**
- You care but avoid hard truths to "be nice"
- Hurts people long-term by letting problems fester
- Example: "Great job on the presentation!" (when it actually wasn't)

**Obnoxious Aggression (Challenge, No Care)**
- Direct feedback without caring about the person
- Feels like personal attack
- Example: "That presentation was terrible. Do better."

**Manipulative Insincerity (No Care, No Challenge)**
- Neither care nor honesty
- Passive-aggressive or political
- Example: Complaining to others but saying nothing to the person

### How to Use It
1. Build relationships first (care personally)
2. Then challenge directly with kindness
3. Focus on behavior, not personality
4. Make it about helping them succeed

## SBI Model (Situation-Behavior-Impact)

### The Three Components

**Situation** - When and where did this happen?
- Be specific about context
- "In yesterday's standup..."
- "During the Q4 planning meeting..."

**Behavior** - What exactly did they do or say?
- Observable actions, not interpretations
- "You interrupted Sarah three times..."
- "You shipped the feature without tests..."

**Impact** - What was the effect?
- On the team, project, customers, or them
- "...which made it hard for the team to share updates"
- "...which caused production bugs and customer complaints"

### SBI Examples

**Positive Feedback:**
> "In yesterday's design review (S), you asked really clarifying questions about edge cases (B), which helped us identify a critical bug before we built it (I). That saved us days of rework. Great work."

**Constructive Feedback:**
> "In this morning's standup (S), you said 'this is stupid' when discussing the new process (B). This made the team uncomfortable and shut down discussion (I). I know you have concerns - can we talk about them in a way that invites collaboration?"

**Career Development:**
> "Over the past three months (S), you've been taking on mentor relationships with junior engineers (B). This has really accelerated their growth and freed up senior eng time (I). I'd like to talk about formalizing this as part of your role."

### When to Use SBI
- ✓ Straightforward situations
- ✓ First-time feedback
- ✓ Documenting for reviews
- ✗ Complex interpersonal issues (use COIN instead)

## COIN Model (Context-Observation-Impact-Next Steps)

### The Four Components

**Context** - Set the scene
- More detailed than SBI's "situation"
- Include relevant background
- "I want to talk about our working relationship on the auth project..."

**Observation** - What did you observe?
- Focus on facts, not judgments
- "I've noticed that deadlines have been missed three times this month"
- Can include patterns over time

**Impact** - What's the effect?
- Personal, team, business impact
- Include emotions when relevant
- "This has made it hard to plan dependent work and has frustrated the frontend team"

**Next Steps** - What should happen going forward?
- Collaborative problem-solving
- Ask for their input
- Agree on specific actions

### COIN Examples

**Performance Issue:**
> **Context**: "I want to talk about the API project that's due next week."
> **Observation**: "I've noticed that you haven't committed any code in the past week, and when I asked for an update yesterday, you said you'd get back to me but didn't."
> **Impact**: "This makes me worried we won't hit our deadline, and the mobile team is blocked waiting for this API."
> **Next Steps**: "Can you help me understand what's happening? What obstacles are you facing? How can we get this back on track?"

**Interpersonal Conflict:**
> **Context**: "I want to discuss what happened in the team meeting yesterday."
> **Observation**: "When Jamie suggested a different approach, you rolled your eyes and said 'that'll never work' without explaining why."
> **Impact**: "Jamie looked hurt and stopped participating. I'm concerned this is affecting psychological safety on the team."
> **Next Steps**: "I know you had concerns about the approach. Can we talk about better ways to express disagreement? I'd like you to apologize to Jamie and then we can discuss the technical concerns properly."

### When to Use COIN
- ✓ Complex situations
- ✓ Patterns of behavior
- ✓ Performance conversations
- ✓ When you need collaboration to solve it

## Feedback Spectrum

### Positive Feedback (Reinforcement)
**Purpose:** Encourage repetition of good behavior

**Do:**
- Be specific about what was good
- Explain the impact
- Give it publicly (when appropriate)
- Give it immediately

**Example:**
"That's the third time this month you've volunteered to help onboard new team members. Your detailed documentation and patience make our ramp-up time 50% faster. Thank you - this is exactly the kind of team citizenship we value."

### Constructive Feedback (Development)
**Purpose:** Help someone improve

**Do:**
- Be direct but kind
- Focus on behavior, not personality
- Provide examples
- Suggest alternatives

**Example:**
"In code reviews, I've noticed you sometimes say 'this is wrong' without explanation. This can feel dismissive to the author. Try something like 'I have concerns about this approach because...' and then explain your reasoning."

### Critical Feedback (Course Correction)
**Purpose:** Stop harmful behavior immediately

**Do:**
- Give it quickly and privately
- Be very direct
- Explain serious consequences
- Require acknowledgment

**Example:**
"We need to talk right now. In that client call, you contradicted me in front of the customer about our timeline without discussing it with me first. This damaged our credibility and put the deal at risk. This can't happen again. If you have concerns about timelines, we need to discuss them before the call."

## The Feedback Equation

```
Effective Feedback = Specificity × Timeliness × Actionability × Care
```

If any component is zero, the feedback fails.

## Delivery Techniques

### Feedback Sandwich (Use Sparingly)
Structure: Positive → Constructive → Positive

**Why it's problematic:**
- People learn to fear positive feedback
- The constructive part gets lost
- Feels manipulative when overused

**When to use it:**
- Very junior or sensitive team members
- First time giving someone feedback
- Very minor issues

**Better approach:** Give positive and constructive feedback separately

### Ask-Don't Tell Approach

Instead of: "You need to improve your communication"
Try: "How do you think that meeting went? What might you do differently?"

**Benefits:**
- Creates self-awareness
- Reduces defensiveness
- Develops critical thinking
- They own the solution

### Feedforward (Marshall Goldsmith)

Focus on future behavior, not past mistakes:
- "Going forward, try..."
- "Next time, consider..."
- "I'd like to see you..."

**Why it works:**
- Less defensive than dwelling on past
- Action-oriented
- Positive framing

## Difficult Feedback Scenarios

### Hygiene Issues
Be direct and private:
> "This is awkward, but I need to tell you that there's been a body odor issue. I'm telling you because I care about your success here, and I don't want this to affect how people perceive you. Can we figure out a solution?"

### Personality Conflicts
Focus on behaviors and impact:
> "I've noticed tension between you and Alex. Can you help me understand what's happening? Regardless of personal feelings, we need to work together professionally. What specifically needs to change?"

### Poor Work Quality
Be specific with examples:
> "The last three PRs had bugs that were caught in production. Let's look at them together. What's making it hard to catch these before shipping? Do you need more testing time? Pairing? Code review depth?"

### Lack of Self-Awareness
Use questions and reflection:
> "You mentioned you want to be promoted to senior, but I've observed that you don't ask for help when stuck and you've missed deadlines. Do you see the connection? What would need to change?"

## Receiving Feedback

### As a Manager, Model Good Behavior

**Ask for feedback regularly:**
- "What's one thing I could do better?"
- "How can I support you more effectively?"
- "What should I stop/start/continue doing?"

**When receiving feedback:**
- Listen without defending
- Thank them for the feedback
- Ask clarifying questions
- Follow up with actions taken

**Example response:**
"Thank you for telling me I've been interrupting in meetings. That's not okay and I didn't realize I was doing it. I'm going to work on that. Please call me out if I do it again."

## Feedback Cadence

| Type | Frequency |
|------|-----------|
| Positive reinforcement | Immediately, often |
| Minor constructive | Within 24-48 hours |
| Significant issues | Same day, privately |
| Pattern feedback | After 2-3 instances |
| Formal review | Quarterly/annually |

## Common Mistakes

### ❌ The Feedback Dump
- Saving up months of feedback
- Overwhelming them with issues
- **Fix:** Give feedback continuously

### ❌ Vague Feedback
- "You need to be more professional"
- "Your code quality needs work"
- **Fix:** Use specific examples (SBI/COIN)

### ❌ Secondhand Feedback
- "Some people have said..."
- "I heard that you..."
- **Fix:** Only give firsthand feedback

### ❌ Feedback in Anger
- Reacting emotionally
- Attacking the person
- **Fix:** Wait 24 hours, then use a framework

### ❌ No Follow-up
- Give feedback and forget
- Don't check for improvement
- **Fix:** Schedule follow-up discussion

## Feedback Culture

### Building a Feedback-Rich Team

1. **Model it** - Give and receive feedback openly
2. **Normalize it** - Make it routine, not scary
3. **Train it** - Teach frameworks like SBI
4. **Celebrate it** - Thank people for feedback
5. **Act on it** - Show feedback leads to change

### Team Feedback Practices

- **Retrospectives** - Team-level feedback
- **360 reviews** - Multi-directional feedback
- **Peer feedback** - Normalize lateral feedback
- **Skip levels** - Feedback about managers

## Scripts and Templates

### Opening Scripts

**For positive feedback:**
"Hey, I want to share something I noticed..."

**For constructive feedback:**
"Can we talk? I have some feedback that I think will help you."

**For critical feedback:**
"We need to discuss something important right now."

### Follow-up Template

```
Following up on our conversation about [topic]:

What we discussed:
- [Summary of feedback]

What you committed to:
- [Specific actions]

How I'll support you:
- [Your commitments]

Let's check in again on [date].
```
