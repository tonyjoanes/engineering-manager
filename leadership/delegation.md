# Delegation Framework

The art of empowering your team while scaling your impact.

## Why Delegation Matters

**Without delegation, you:**
- Become the bottleneck
- Limit team growth
- Burn yourself out
- Cap team's potential
- Can't take on strategic work

**With effective delegation:**
- Scale your impact through others
- Develop your team's skills
- Free up time for high-leverage work
- Build trust and engagement
- Prepare people for advancement

**Your job as manager:** Make yourself obsolete in operational work.

## The Delegation Mindset Shift

### From Doer to Multiplier

**Maker mentality (IC):**
- "I can do this faster myself"
- Quality = me doing it
- Success = my output

**Manager mentality:**
- "This is a learning opportunity"
- Quality = team capability
- Success = team's output

### Common Delegation Fears

**"They'll do it wrong"**
- Reality: They'll do it differently (not necessarily wrong)
- Growth requires mistakes
- Your way isn't the only way

**"It's faster if I do it"**
- True for one task, false for career
- Short-term thinking
- Robs team of growth

**"They're too busy"**
- They're busy with low-value work
- Delegation reshuffles priorities
- Ask them, don't assume

**"I'll look lazy"**
- Your job is team output, not your output
- Strategic work is harder to see but more valuable
- Delegation is leadership, not laziness

## What to Delegate

### Delegation Decision Matrix

```
                 High Impact
                      ↑
        Develop    |  Strategize
        (Delegate) |  (Keep)
    ←───────────────┼───────────────→
        Eliminate  |  Execute
        (Stop)     |  (Delegate)
                   ↓
                Low Impact
```

### Should Delegate

**Operational tasks:**
- Routine reports
- Status updates
- Meeting notes
- Code reviews (share the load)
- Bug triage
- Monitoring dashboards

**Growth opportunities:**
- Challenging projects
- Presentations
- New technologies
- Leadership opportunities
- Cross-team collaboration

**Tasks others can do 70% as well:**
- If they can do it 70% as well as you, delegate
- They'll get to 100% with practice
- Holding on prevents their growth

### Should NOT Delegate

**Manager responsibilities:**
- Performance reviews
- Compensation decisions
- Hiring/firing decisions
- Sensitive HR matters
- Setting team vision
- Your manager's asks of you

**Requires specific context:**
- Tasks requiring your relationships
- Confidential matters
- Executive-level strategy

**Things you want to learn:**
- New strategic areas
- Skills you're developing
- Building key relationships

## The Delegation Process

### Step 1: Choose the Right Person

**Consider:**
- **Skill match** - Do they have the capability?
- **Development opportunity** - Will this help them grow?
- **Capacity** - Do they have time?
- **Interest** - Is this aligned with their goals?
- **Readiness** - Are they ready for this stretch?

**Don't always pick:**
- The person who always says yes
- The highest performer for everything
- The person most like you

**Do consider:**
- Rotating opportunities
- Stretch assignments
- Aligning with career goals

### Step 2: Provide Context

**The "Why, What, How" framework:**

**Why (Purpose):**
- Why does this matter?
- What's the business impact?
- How does it connect to goals?
- Why are we doing it now?

**What (Outcome):**
- What does success look like?
- What are the constraints?
- What's the deadline?
- What's in/out of scope?

**How (Approach):**
- How much autonomy do they have?
- How should they make decisions?
- How often should we check in?
- How do they get help?

### Step 3: Establish Appropriate Level of Delegation

**The 7 Levels of Delegation:**

**1. Do what I say** (lowest autonomy)
- Very specific instructions
- For critical/high-risk tasks or very junior people

**2. Research and report, I'll decide**
- Gather information
- Present options
- You make the call

**3. Research and recommend, I'll decide**
- Do analysis
- Make recommendation
- You approve or veto

**4. Decide, but check with me first**
- They decide
- Run it by you before executing
- You rarely override

**5. Decide and inform me** (highest common level)
- They decide
- Tell you what they decided
- You're informed, not approving

**6. Decide and act, report results**
- Full autonomy
- Update you afterward
- For trusted team, routine work

**7. Complete autonomy**
- They own it fully
- You might not even know details
- Rare, for very senior people

**Match level to:**
- Risk of decision
- Person's experience
- Time sensitivity
- Learning opportunity

### Step 4: Provide Resources

**What they need:**
- Access to systems/data
- Introduction to stakeholders
- Budget authority (if needed)
- Time allocation
- Documentation/examples
- Your availability for questions

**Set them up for success:**
"Here's who can help with X..."
"This document has context on..."
"Block time on your calendar for this..."

### Step 5: Agree on Check-ins

**Establish cadence:**
- How often will we sync?
- What format (async update, meeting)?
- What triggers an early check-in?
- How do they escalate if blocked?

**Examples:**
- "Send me a weekly update every Friday"
- "Let's have 15-min sync every Monday"
- "Message me if you're stuck for more than 2 hours"
- "Monthly review of metrics"

**Balance:**
- Too frequent = micromanagement
- Too rare = abandonment
- Right amount = support + autonomy

### Step 6: Let Go

**After delegating:**
- Trust them to own it
- Don't hover or second-guess
- Let them solve problems
- Resist urge to "fix" everything
- Accept their approach may differ

**When to intervene:**
- About to make serious mistake
- Clearly stuck and not asking for help
- Missing deadlines without communicating
- Scope creep that affects other work

**When NOT to intervene:**
- Doing it differently than you would
- Minor inefficiencies
- Learning opportunities
- Your discomfort with not controlling

### Step 7: Support and Coach

**Be available:**
- Answer questions promptly
- Unblock obstacles
- Provide guidance when asked
- Celebrate progress

**Coach, don't do:**
❌ "Let me just do it"
✓ "What approaches have you considered?"

❌ "Here's exactly what to do"
✓ "What do you think you should do?"

❌ "You're doing it wrong"
✓ "I'm noticing X. What's your thinking?"

### Step 8: Review and Learn

**After completion:**
- Celebrate the win
- Debrief what went well
- Discuss what was hard
- Identify learnings
- Thank them explicitly

**Reflection questions:**
- What surprised you?
- What would you do differently?
- What support was most helpful?
- What did you learn?
- What would you like to do next?

## Delegation Scenarios

### Scenario: First-Time Delegation

**Task:** Lead the sprint planning meeting

**Script:**
"I'd like you to facilitate next sprint's planning meeting. Here's why: you have great facilitation skills and this would help you grow into more leadership roles.

What it involves: Running the 2-hour session, ensuring we prioritize the backlog, keeping us on track, and sending out the summary afterwards.

I'll be there to support, but you'll be leading. We can prep together the day before. I'll give you feedback afterward.

How does that sound? Any questions?"

### Scenario: Delegating Up (Your Senior)

**Task:** Have them present to leadership

**Script:**
"You did the deep work on this project and understand it better than anyone. I'd like you to present our findings to the VP.

This is a great visibility opportunity for you and your work deserves the recognition. I'll be in the meeting to support, but you'll take the lead.

Let's prep together. I'll share what the VP cares about most and how to frame the narrative. Would you be up for this?"

### Scenario: Delegation Recovery

**When someone struggles:**

**Don't:**
❌ Take it back immediately
❌ Criticize publicly
❌ Do it for them

**Do:**
✓ "I notice X is challenging. Let's talk about it."
✓ "What support would help?"
✓ "Let's pair on this together."
✓ Provide more structure if needed

## Resistance to Delegation

### From Team Members

**"I'm too busy"**
- Response: "Let's look at your priorities together. What can we deprioritize?"
- May be true - need to clear their plate first

**"I don't know how"**
- Response: "This is a learning opportunity. I'll support you. Where should we start?"
- Provide training, resources, pairing

**"I'll probably mess it up"**
- Response: "I trust you can handle this. We'll check in regularly and adjust as needed."
- Build confidence through support

**"Why can't you just do it?"**
- Response: "This is important for your growth. Also, we need to scale beyond just me."
- Explain development rationale

### From Yourself

**Perfectionism:**
- Good enough is good enough
- Perfect is the enemy of done
- Your way isn't the only way

**Control issues:**
- Trust your hiring
- Mistakes are learning opportunities
- Delegation is leadership

**Impostor syndrome:**
- You're not "dumping" work, you're developing people
- Your job is to enable others, not do everything
- Delegation increases your value

## Micromanagement vs Abdication

### Micromanagement (Too Much Control)

**Signs:**
- Require approval for every decision
- Check in multiple times daily
- Override their decisions regularly
- Prescribe exact methods
- Don't trust their judgment

**Impact:**
- Demotivates team
- Slows everything down
- Prevents growth
- People stop trying

### Abdication (Too Little Support)

**Signs:**
- No context provided
- Unclear expectations
- Never check in
- Unavailable for questions
- Ignore warning signs

**Impact:**
- People feel abandoned
- Projects fail
- Trust erodes
- "Thrown in deep end"

### The Right Balance

**Characteristics:**
- Clear expectations
- Appropriate autonomy
- Regular check-ins
- Available when needed
- Trust with verification
- Support without hovering

## Delegation by Experience Level

### Junior Team Members

**What to delegate:**
- Well-defined tasks
- Lower-risk work
- Learning opportunities
- Pair with senior for complex work

**How:**
- More structure
- Frequent check-ins
- Explicit instructions
- Lots of support

**Level:** 1-3 on delegation scale

### Mid-Level Team Members

**What to delegate:**
- Feature ownership
- Small projects
- Some ambiguous work
- Mentorship of juniors

**How:**
- Moderate structure
- Bi-weekly check-ins
- Outcome-focused
- Coach when they ask

**Level:** 3-5 on delegation scale

### Senior Team Members

**What to delegate:**
- Strategic projects
- High ambiguity work
- Architecture decisions
- Cross-team initiatives

**How:**
- Minimal structure
- Monthly check-ins
- Trust their judgment
- Mostly hands-off

**Level:** 5-7 on delegation scale

## Tracking What You've Delegated

### Keep a Delegation Log

**Track:**
- What was delegated
- To whom
- When
- Expected completion
- Check-in schedule
- Outcome

**Why:**
- Don't forget what you delegated
- Ensure even distribution
- Track development opportunities
- Follow up appropriately

**Simple format:**
```
| Task | Owner | Delegated | Due | Next Check-in | Status |
|------|-------|-----------|-----|---------------|--------|
| Sprint planning | Alex | 2024-01 | Ongoing | Weekly | ✓ |
| Q1 Metrics | Jamie | 2024-01 | 2024-03-31 | Monthly | In progress |
```

## Measuring Delegation Success

**Good signs:**
- Your calendar has strategic work blocked
- Team is growing in capability
- You're not the bottleneck
- People ask for more responsibility
- You can take vacation without issues

**Warning signs:**
- Everything still comes to you
- Team waiting for your decisions
- You're working nights and weekends
- People not asking for more work
- Projects failing when you're not involved

## Common Delegation Mistakes

### ❌ Delegating at the Last Minute
- Not enough time to do it well
- **Fix:** Delegate early in the timeline

### ❌ Delegating Only the Boring Stuff
- Demotivating
- **Fix:** Delegate interesting challenges too

### ❌ Vague Instructions
- "Make the performance better"
- **Fix:** Clear success criteria

### ❌ Taking It Back at First Struggle
- Robs them of learning
- **Fix:** Support through difficulty

### ❌ Not Following Up
- Abandonment
- **Fix:** Scheduled check-ins

### ❌ Public Criticism
- Embarrasses and demotivates
- **Fix:** Private feedback, public praise

## Reverse Delegation (Monkey Management)

### What It Is
- Team member comes with problem
- You end up owning it
- They delegated UP to you

**Example conversation:**
Them: "The deployment is broken, what should I do?"
You: "Let me look at it" ← You just took the monkey

### How to Prevent It

**Use the 5 Questions:**

1. **"What do you think we should do?"**
   - Make them think first

2. **"What are the options?"**
   - Develop problem-solving skills

3. **"What are the pros and cons?"**
   - Evaluate trade-offs

4. **"What do you recommend?"**
   - Make them commit to a solution

5. **"Sounds good. Go ahead and do that. Let me know how it goes."**
   - Put ownership back on them

**Exception:** True emergencies or areas you own

## Delegation Conversation Template

```
"[Name], I'd like to delegate [task] to you.

Why: [Business importance + growth opportunity for them]

What success looks like: [Specific outcomes and constraints]

Your authority: [Decision-making level]

Resources available: [People, docs, budget, etc.]

Timeline: [Deadline and milestones]

Check-ins: [How often and format]

This is important because [context and impact].

What questions do you have? Does this seem doable given your current workload?"
```
