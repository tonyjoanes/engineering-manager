# Hiring & Interviewing

A comprehensive guide to building great teams through effective hiring practices.

## The Cost of Bad Hires

**A bad hire costs you:**
- 6-12 months of salary (recruiting, training, severance)
- Team productivity and morale
- Manager time spent on performance management
- Opportunity cost of projects delayed
- Cultural damage if behavior is toxic

**Take hiring seriously** - It's one of your highest leverage activities.

## Before You Start: Define the Role

### Create a Hiring Brief

**Must have before posting:**
- **Role title and level** - Be accurate about seniority
- **Core responsibilities** - What they'll actually do day-to-day
- **Required skills** - Technical must-haves
- **Preferred skills** - Nice-to-haves (don't make list too long)
- **Team context** - Who they'll work with, projects they'll join
- **Success metrics** - What does good look like at 3/6/12 months?
- **Growth opportunities** - Career path from this role

### Define Your Bar

**What are you optimizing for?**
- Technical depth vs breadth
- Individual contributor vs collaboration skills
- Building new things vs maintaining existing
- Autonomy vs mentorship needs
- Culture add vs culture fit

**Write down your "must haves":**
- 3-5 non-negotiable skills/qualities
- Everything else is negotiable
- Helps prevent "perfect candidate" syndrome

## Sourcing Candidates

### Channels

**Best sources (in order):**
1. **Internal referrals** - Pre-vetted, cultural fit known
2. **Your network** - People you've worked with
3. **Passive candidates** - Reach out directly (LinkedIn)
4. **Job boards** - Cast wide net
5. **Recruiters** - For hard-to-fill roles

### Referral Programs

**Make it easy:**
- Clear job descriptions
- Referral bonus structure
- Simple submission process
- Regular updates on referred candidates

**Incentivize quality over quantity:**
- Bonus on hire AND after 6-month retention
- Public recognition
- Track referrer as "source"

### Writing Job Descriptions

**Do:**
✓ Start with why the role matters
✓ Be specific about day-to-day work
✓ List 5-7 must-have qualifications
✓ Describe your team culture
✓ Include salary range (transparency helps)
✓ Keep it concise (300-500 words)

**Don't:**
✗ List 20 requirements
✗ Use jargon and buzzwords
✗ Make every skill "required"
✗ Copy-paste from other companies
✗ Make it boring

**Good example:**
"You'll build the API that powers our mobile app, used by 2M users daily. You'll work with a team of 4 engineers who care about code quality, mentorship, and work-life balance. We're looking for someone who's built production APIs at scale and wants to solve challenging distributed systems problems."

## Interview Process Design

### Standard Process Structure

**Typical flow:**
1. **Recruiter screen (30 min)** - Basic fit, logistics, comp expectations
2. **Hiring manager screen (45 min)** - Culture, experience, motivation
3. **Technical assessment** - Varies by role
4. **Onsite/Virtual interviews (3-5 hours)** - Deep technical + team fit
5. **Reference checks** - For finalists only
6. **Offer** - Decision within 48 hours of final interview

### Interview Loop Design

**Typical onsite/virtual structure (4-5 interviews):**

1. **Coding/Technical (60-90 min)**
   - Assess core technical skills
   - Problem-solving approach
   - Communication while coding

2. **System Design (60 min)**
   - Architecture thinking
   - Scalability considerations
   - Trade-off analysis

3. **Behavioral/Experience (60 min)**
   - Past work and impact
   - Collaboration and communication
   - Learning and growth

4. **Team Collaboration (45 min)**
   - Working with team members
   - Cross-functional skills
   - Cultural fit

5. **Hiring Manager Final (30 min)**
   - Questions from candidate
   - Sell the role
   - Logistics and next steps

**Key principles:**
- Each interview tests something different
- No redundant questions
- Mix of technical and interpersonal
- Include diverse interviewers
- Leave time for candidate questions

## Conducting Effective Interviews

### Before the Interview

**Prepare:**
- Read resume thoroughly
- Review hiring brief and bar
- Prepare specific questions
- Have clear evaluation criteria
- Test any tech (for virtual interviews)

**Mindset:**
- You're selling as much as evaluating
- Candidate is interviewing you too
- Look for reasons to hire, not reject
- Check your biases

### Interview Structure (60 min)

**Opening (5 min)**
- Introduce yourself and role
- Explain interview structure
- Put them at ease
- "Any questions before we start?"

**Main Assessment (45 min)**
- Ask prepared questions
- Dig deeper with follow-ups
- Take notes throughout
- Watch for red flags and green flags

**Their Questions (10 min)**
- Leave real time for this
- Answer honestly
- Sell the role and team
- Address concerns

**Close (2 min)**
- Explain next steps
- Timeline for decision
- Thank them

### Asking Good Questions

**Behavioral Questions (STAR Method)**

Structure: Situation, Task, Action, Result

**Bad:** "Are you a good communicator?"
**Good:** "Tell me about a time you had to explain a complex technical concept to a non-technical stakeholder. What was the situation, what did you do, and what was the outcome?"

**Good questions:**
- "Tell me about your most impactful project. What made it impactful?"
- "Describe a time when you disagreed with your team about a technical decision. How did you handle it?"
- "Tell me about a time you failed. What happened and what did you learn?"
- "Walk me through a system you built from scratch. What were the challenges?"

**Technical Questions**

**For problem-solving:**
- Start with easier problem, scale up complexity
- Watch HOW they think, not just the answer
- Ask them to explain their approach
- See how they handle hints and feedback

**For system design:**
- Start vague, let them ask clarifying questions
- Watch how they handle ambiguity
- See how they make trade-offs
- Look for pragmatism vs over-engineering

**For past work:**
- "Tell me about the most complex system you've worked on"
- "What was your role in [project on resume]?"
- "How did you handle [technical challenge]?"
- "What would you do differently if you could redo [project]?"

### What to Assess

**Technical Skills**
- Core programming/technical ability
- Problem-solving approach
- System design thinking
- Code quality and best practices
- Learning ability

**Collaboration Skills**
- Communication clarity
- Asking good questions
- Giving and receiving feedback
- Working with others
- Handling disagreement

**Motivation & Fit**
- Why this role/company?
- What energizes them?
- Career goals alignment
- Learning orientation
- Values alignment

**Red Flags to Watch For**
- Blaming others constantly
- Can't explain their own work
- Arrogance or condescension
- Not asking any questions
- Obvious resume exaggeration
- Communication problems

**Green Flags to Look For**
- Specific examples with details
- Shows ownership and initiative
- Learns from mistakes
- Asks thoughtful questions
- Communicates clearly
- Excited about the work

## Avoiding Bias

### Common Biases in Hiring

**Affinity bias** - Preferring people like you
**Halo effect** - One positive trait colors everything
**Horn effect** - One negative trait colors everything
**Recency bias** - Most recent candidate seems best
**Confirmation bias** - Looking for evidence to support first impression
**Contrast effect** - Comparing to previous candidate, not the bar

### Mitigation Strategies

**1. Structured Interviews**
- Same questions for all candidates
- Consistent evaluation criteria
- Reduces improvisation bias

**2. Diverse Interview Panel**
- Different backgrounds and perspectives
- Reduces groupthink
- Better assessment of fit

**3. Evaluation Rubric**
- Score each dimension independently
- Write down evidence before discussing
- Don't anchor on first opinion shared

**4. Skills-Based Assessment**
- Focus on work samples and abilities
- Not pedigree (school, previous companies)
- Can they do the job?

**5. Pause and Check**
- "Why do I think this?"
- "Am I comparing to bar or to last candidate?"
- "What would make me change my mind?"

## Evaluation & Decision Making

### Scoring System

Use consistent rubric for each interview segment:

**Scale:**
- **Strong Yes (4)** - Exceeds bar, would be excited to work with
- **Yes (3)** - Meets bar, would be good addition
- **No (2)** - Below bar in some areas
- **Strong No (1)** - Significantly below bar

**For each area assessed:**
- Score independently
- Write specific evidence
- Identify strengths and concerns

### Debrief Meeting

**Structure:**
1. **Independent submission first** - Everyone scores before meeting
2. **Go around the room** - Each person shares assessment
3. **Discuss concerns** - Address specific issues
4. **Check for bias** - Are we being fair?
5. **Make decision** - Hire/No hire/Re-interview

**Red flags in debrief:**
- "I just have a feeling..." (no evidence)
- "They remind me of..." (bias)
- "They went to X school" (pedigree, not skills)
- "They seem smart" (halo effect)

**Good signals:**
- Specific examples cited
- Consistent feedback across interviews
- Clear connection to bar
- Discussion of trade-offs

### Making the Decision

**Hire when:**
- Meets or exceeds bar on core requirements
- No major red flags
- Would raise team quality
- Team is excited

**Don't hire when:**
- Below bar on must-haves (even if great elsewhere)
- Significant red flags
- Lukewarm team response
- "We can fix that" thinking

**When in doubt:**
- Err on side of no hire
- Better to keep looking than bad hire
- Re-interview if you need more signal

**"Raise the bar" hiring:**
- Each hire should be better than 50% of current team in their area
- Prevents quality drift downward

## Reference Checks

### When to Do Them
- After you've decided you want to hire
- Before extending offer
- Not worth time for candidates you won't hire

### Who to Call
- Ask candidate for references (usually 2-3)
- Try to find back-channel references (people you know who worked with them)
- Prioritize recent managers and peers

### Questions to Ask

**Performance:**
- "What was [name]'s role on your team?"
- "What were their biggest strengths?"
- "What areas were they working to develop?"
- "How did they compare to others in that role?"

**Collaboration:**
- "How did they work with others?"
- "How did they handle conflict or disagreement?"
- "Would your team want to work with them again?"

**The key question:**
- "Would you hire them again if you could?"
- "On a scale of 1-10, how strongly would you recommend them?"

**Listen for:**
- Enthusiasm level
- Specific examples
- Unprompted positives
- How they answer "areas to develop"
- What they DON'T say

### Red Flags in References
- Can't provide specific examples
- Lukewarm or generic praise
- Hesitation on "would you hire again?"
- Focus on personality over performance
- Damning with faint praise

## Making the Offer

### Components

**Financial:**
- Base salary (match market rate)
- Equity/stock options (if applicable)
- Bonus structure
- Sign-on bonus (if needed)

**Non-financial:**
- Role and level
- Team and manager
- Start date
- Benefits summary
- Remote/hybrid policy

### Offer Conversation

**Do:**
- Call them directly (don't just email)
- Express enthusiasm
- Explain the offer components
- Give them time to consider
- Address questions honestly

**Don't:**
- Pressure for immediate answer
- Lowball on compensation
- Promise things you can't deliver
- Ghost them after offer

**Script:**
"We're excited to offer you the [role] position. Here's the package: [details]. I know this is a big decision. Take the time you need. I'm here to answer any questions. What questions do you have right now?"

### Negotiation

**Expect negotiation** - Good candidates will negotiate

**What you can negotiate:**
- Salary (within approved range)
- Sign-on bonus (to offset equity from previous company)
- Start date
- Remote work arrangements
- Sometimes level (if clearly justified)

**What's usually fixed:**
- Equity (tied to level)
- Benefits
- Core policies

**How to handle:**
- Don't be offended
- Understand their constraints
- Find creative solutions
- Know your walk-away point
- Get approvals for non-standard terms

## Closing Candidates

### Why People Say No

Common reasons:
- Compensation not competitive
- Unclear about role or growth
- Better offer elsewhere
- Concerns about team/manager
- Life circumstances
- Counter-offer from current company

### Selling the Role

**What candidates care about (in order):**
1. **Learning & growth** - Will I develop new skills?
2. **Impact** - Will my work matter?
3. **Team** - Will I like working with these people?
4. **Compensation** - Am I paid fairly?
5. **Work-life balance** - Can I have a life?
6. **Mission** - Do I care about the product?

**Selling techniques:**
- Share specific growth opportunities
- Highlight impact potential
- Let them meet the team
- Be transparent about challenges
- Address concerns directly

### Handling Counter-Offers

**When candidate gets counter-offer:**

"I understand you got a counter-offer. That's a sign they value you. Here are some questions to consider:
- Why did it take you looking elsewhere to get this offer?
- Does it address why you were looking?
- Where do you see yourself in a year?"

**If they take the counter:**
- Thank them graciously
- Keep door open
- Stay in touch

**Stats:** 70-80% of people who accept counter-offers leave within 6 months anyway.

## After the Hire

### First Day Preparation

**Before they start:**
- Equipment ready
- Accounts created
- Onboarding plan prepared
- Welcome email sent
- Team notified

See [Onboarding Playbook](./onboarding.md) for details.

### Setting Them Up for Success

**First week:**
- Intro to team
- Dev environment setup
- First small task
- 1:1 scheduled

**First month:**
- Real project to work on
- Regular check-ins
- Pair programming
- Feedback early and often

**First 90 days:**
- Increasing autonomy
- Full contributor
- First performance conversation

## Common Hiring Mistakes

### ❌ Hiring for Pedigree Over Skills
- Focusing on school or previous companies
- **Fix:** Focus on what they can do

### ❌ Moving Too Fast
- Pressure to fill seat
- Lowering bar due to urgency
- **Fix:** Better to wait for right person

### ❌ Unclear Bar
- Different interviewers assess differently
- No consistent standards
- **Fix:** Define bar before interviewing

### ❌ Gut Feeling Over Evidence
- "I just like them"
- Can't articulate why
- **Fix:** Require specific examples

### ❌ Culture Fit Over Culture Add
- Hiring people just like current team
- **Fix:** Look for different perspectives

### ❌ Selling Too Hard
- Misrepresenting role or company
- Overpromising
- **Fix:** Be honest, even about challenges

## Hiring Different Levels

### Junior Engineers
**Look for:**
- Learning ability
- Fundamentals
- Potential over experience
- Coachability
- Enthusiasm

**Red flags:**
- Know-it-all attitude
- Can't admit what they don't know
- No side projects or curiosity

### Senior Engineers
**Look for:**
- Technical depth
- System design skills
- Mentorship ability
- Project ownership
- Impact focus

**Red flags:**
- Ego, not collaborative
- Can't explain past work
- Blame others for failures

### Staff+ Engineers
**Look for:**
- Technical vision
- Cross-team influence
- Strategic thinking
- Multiplier effect
- Track record of major projects

**Red flags:**
- Only talks about past
- Can't adapt to new context
- Poor communicator

## Interview Question Bank

### Behavioral Questions

**Leadership & Initiative:**
- "Tell me about a time you identified and drove a significant improvement"
- "Describe a project you led. What made it successful or challenging?"
- "Tell me about a time you had to convince others to do something differently"

**Collaboration:**
- "Tell me about a time you had a disagreement with a coworker"
- "Describe your best and worst working relationship. What made them that way?"
- "Tell me about a time you got critical feedback. How did you respond?"

**Problem-Solving:**
- "Tell me about the hardest technical problem you've solved"
- "Describe a time when you had to make a decision with incomplete information"
- "Tell me about a project that failed. What happened?"

**Learning & Growth:**
- "Tell me about a time you learned a new technology quickly"
- "What's the biggest mistake you made and what did you learn?"
- "How do you stay current with technology?"

### Technical Questions

**System Design:**
- "Design a URL shortener like bit.ly"
- "Design Instagram's news feed"
- "Design a rate limiter"
- "Design a distributed cache"

**Coding:**
- String manipulation
- Array/list operations
- Tree traversal
- Graph problems
- Dynamic programming (for senior+)

### Closing Questions

**Understanding Motivation:**
- "Why are you looking to leave your current role?"
- "What are you looking for in your next role?"
- "Why this company/team?"
- "Where do you see yourself in 3-5 years?"

**Assessing Fit:**
- "What kind of manager do you work best with?"
- "What does your ideal day look like?"
- "What energizes you? What drains you?"
