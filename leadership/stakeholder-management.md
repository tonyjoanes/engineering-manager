# Stakeholder Management

Building relationships and managing expectations across the organization.

## Who Are Your Stakeholders?

**Internal:**
- Your manager
- Peer managers
- Other engineering teams
- Product managers
- Designers
- Data scientists
- Leadership/executives
- HR, Finance, Legal

**External:**
- Customers (sometimes)
- Partners
- Vendors

**Your team is also a stakeholder!**

## Why Stakeholder Management Matters

**Good stakeholder management:**
- Smoother project delivery
- Better resource allocation
- Influence and support
- Career advancement
- Reduced friction

**Poor stakeholder management:**
- Projects blocked
- Misaligned priorities
- Surprises and conflicts
- Limited influence
- Career stagnation

## Understanding Stakeholders

### Stakeholder Mapping

**Power/Interest Matrix:**

```
           High Interest
                ↑
     Manage    | Partner
     Closely   | Closely
←──────────────┼──────────────→
     Monitor   | Keep
             | Informed
                ↓
           Low Interest
```

**Manage Closely:** High power, low interest
- Executives who control resources
- Keep satisfied, don't burden
- High-level updates

**Partner Closely:** High power, high interest
- Your manager
- Key stakeholders
- Frequent communication
- Involve in decisions

**Keep Informed:** Low power, high interest
- Peer teams
- Individual contributors on other teams
- Regular updates
- Answer questions

**Monitor:** Low power, low interest
- Minimal communication
- General broadcasts

### Stakeholder Analysis

**For each key stakeholder, understand:**
- What do they care about?
- What are their goals?
- What success looks like to them?
- What keeps them up at night?
- How do they prefer to communicate?
- What's their power/influence?

## Managing Up (Your Manager)

### Your Manager's Needs

**They need you to:**
- Deliver reliably
- Communicate proactively
- Solve problems
- Make them look good
- Not surprise them
- Support their goals

### Effective Communication

**Weekly updates:**
- What you accomplished
- What's coming next
- Risks/blockers
- Help needed

**Template:**
```
Weekly Update - [Date]

Highlights:
- Shipped X feature
- Team velocity improved
- Hired new engineer

Next Week:
- Planning Q2 roadmap
- Performance reviews
- Architecture review

Risks/Blockers:
- Dependency on Team Y delayed
- Need approval for [decision]

Help Needed:
- Your input on [topic]
- Intro to [person]
```

### Managing Up Tips

**Do:**
✓ Bring solutions, not just problems
✓ Communicate bad news early
✓ Ask for feedback
✓ Align with their goals
✓ Make their job easier

**Don't:**
✗ Surprise them in public
✗ Complain without proposing solutions
✗ Go around them
✗ Make commitments without checking
✗ Ignore their priorities

## Managing Across (Peer Teams)

### Building Relationships

**Invest early:**
- Coffee chats
- Learn their work
- Understand their challenges
- Find common ground
- Help when you can

**Before you need them:**
- Build goodwill
- Establish trust
- Create reciprocity
- Open communication channels

### Working with Product

**Common tensions:**
- Speed vs quality
- Features vs tech debt
- Timelines and estimates
- Scope creep

**How to partner well:**
- Understand their pressures
- Explain technical constraints clearly
- Collaborate on roadmap
- Trade-offs explicitly discussed
- Regular sync meetings
- Shared goals

**Product manager wants:**
- Predictability
- Business impact
- Customer value
- Clear communication
- Partnership in planning

**You want:**
- Reasonable timelines
- Technical debt time
- Clear requirements
- Flexibility for unknowns
- Respect for complexity

**Win-win:**
- Joint roadmap planning
- Balance features and quality
- Transparent trade-offs
- Shared metrics

### Working with Design

**Partnership points:**
- Early involvement (before build)
- Technical feasibility input
- Implementation collaboration
- Respect for each domain
- Iterative approach

### Working with Other Eng Teams

**Dependencies:**
- Early communication
- Clear interfaces
- Shared timelines
- Escalation paths
- Documentation

**Best practices:**
- Overcommunicate
- Weekly syncs if closely coupled
- Slack channel for questions
- Shared architecture reviews
- Help each other

## Managing Expectations

### Set Clear Expectations

**For projects:**
- Scope
- Timeline
- Resources needed
- Risks
- Trade-offs

**For communication:**
- How often?
- What format?
- What level of detail?
- Response time expectations

### Underpromise, Overdeliver

**Buffer estimates:**
- Things take longer than expected
- Unknown unknowns
- Better to deliver early

**Communicate uncertainty:**
"This will take 4-6 weeks, assuming no major blockers"

### Say No When Needed

**You can't do everything:**
- Prioritize ruthlessly
- Explain trade-offs
- Propose alternatives
- Stand firm on capacity

**Script:**
"We can do X or Y this quarter, not both. Here are the trade-offs. Which is more important to the business?"

See [Saying No Gracefully](../heuristics/saying-no.md)

## Influence Without Authority

### Building Influence

**Credibility:**
- Deliver consistently
- Be technically excellent
- Follow through
- Admit mistakes
- Help others

**Relationships:**
- Invest in connections
- Understand others' goals
- Find win-wins
- Build trust

**Communication:**
- Clear and concise
- Data-driven
- Tell good stories
- Adapt to audience

### Persuasion Techniques

**Understand their perspective:**
- What do they care about?
- What are their constraints?
- What's in it for them?

**Frame for your audience:**
- Exec: Business impact, ROI
- Product: User value, metrics
- Engineering: Technical elegance, maintainability
- Design: User experience

**Use data:**
- Metrics and evidence
- Benchmarks
- Customer feedback
- Industry research

**Tell stories:**
- Real examples
- Customer impact
- Team experiences
- Concrete scenarios

## Difficult Stakeholder Scenarios

### The Demanding Stakeholder

**Wants everything now:**

**Response:**
"I understand this is important. Here's our current capacity and priorities. To take this on, what should we deprioritize?"

### The Micromanager

**Questions every decision:**

**Response:**
- Provide visibility proactively
- Explain your process
- Build trust over time
- Set boundaries gently

### The Ghost

**Never responds, then suddenly urgent:**

**Response:**
- Document all communication
- Set deadlines: "If I don't hear by Friday, I'll proceed with X"
- Escalate if blocking work

### The Unrealistic Expectations

**Doesn't understand constraints:**

**Response:**
- Educate on technical realities
- Show trade-offs
- Provide options with pros/cons
- Be patient and persistent

## Communication Best Practices

### Right Channel, Right Message

**Urgent/Important:** Call or Slack DM
**Important/Not Urgent:** Email or scheduled meeting
**FYI:** Email or Slack channel
**Decision:** Doc for input, then meeting

### Executive Communication

**What execs care about:**
- Business impact
- ROI
- Risks
- Strategic alignment
- Bottom line

**How to communicate:**
- Start with conclusion
- Be brief (they're busy)
- Use visuals
- Quantify impact
- Anticipate questions

**Executive update template:**
```
Subject: [Project] Status - [On Track/At Risk/Delayed]

TL;DR: [One sentence summary]

Status: [Green/Yellow/Red]
- Key milestone 1: ✓ Complete
- Key milestone 2: → In progress
- Key milestone 3: → Upcoming

Impact: [Business metric]

Risks:
- [Risk 1]: [Mitigation]

Help Needed:
- [Specific ask]

Details: [Link to full update if they want more]
```

### Proactive Communication

**Don't wait to be asked:**
- Weekly updates
- Early warnings
- Sharing wins
- Heads up on decisions

**Transparency builds trust:**
- Share problems early
- Explain your thinking
- Admit uncertainties
- Show your work

## Building Your Network

### Internal Networking

**Invest in relationships:**
- Coffee chats with peers
- Cross-functional projects
- Lunch and learns
- Slack interactions
- Help others generously

**Expand beyond immediate team:**
- Other engineering teams
- Product, design, data
- Different offices/regions
- Different levels

### Visibility

**Make your work known:**
- Demo days
- Tech talks
- Write documentation
- Share learnings
- Present at all-hands

**Not about bragging:**
- Share credit generously
- Highlight team wins
- Educational, not boastful

## Conflict with Stakeholders

### When There's Disagreement

**Understand their position:**
- What's driving their view?
- What constraints do they have?
- What would change their mind?

**Find common ground:**
- Shared goals
- Mutual interests
- Win-win solutions

**Escalate if needed:**
- Can't resolve at your level
- Document positions
- Let managers align
- Accept decision and move forward

### Protecting Your Team

**When stakeholders pressure team:**

**Shield them:**
- Manage expectations externally
- Filter noise
- Push back on unreasonable demands
- Take the heat yourself

**Don't:**
- Throw team under bus
- Over-commit on their behalf
- Let them be blamed unfairly

## Measuring Success

**Good stakeholder relationships:**
- Projects approved smoothly
- Resources allocated when needed
- Conflicts resolved constructively
- Positive feedback in reviews
- Invited to important meetings
- Influence in decisions
- Career opportunities
