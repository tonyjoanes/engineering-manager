# Building Psychological Safety

Creating an environment where people feel safe to take risks, speak up, and be themselves.

## What is Psychological Safety?

**Definition:** A shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking.

**In practice, people feel they can:**
- Ask questions without seeming ignorant
- Admit mistakes without fear of punishment
- Raise concerns without being shut down
- Propose ideas without ridicule
- Challenge the status quo
- Be themselves without pretending

**What it's NOT:**
- Being nice all the time
- Lowering standards
- Avoiding conflict
- Protecting from consequences

**What it IS:**
- Candor with kindness
- High standards with support
- Productive conflict
- Accountability with empathy

## Why It Matters

**Google's Project Aristotle** found psychological safety is the #1 predictor of high-performing teams.

**With psychological safety:**
- More innovation (people share ideas)
- Better decisions (diverse perspectives heard)
- Faster learning (mistakes shared openly)
- Higher engagement (people bring whole selves)
- Better retention (people want to stay)

**Without it:**
- People stay silent
- Mistakes hidden (until they blow up)
- Innovation stifled
- Talent leaves
- Mediocrity thrives

## The Four Stages of Psychological Safety

### Stage 1: Inclusion Safety
*"I feel accepted and belong here"*

**Behaviors:**
- Greeted warmly
- Included in conversations
- Invited to meetings and social events
- Personal interests acknowledged
- Feel like part of the team

**How to create:**
- Welcome new members warmly
- Use people's names
- Ask about their weekend
- Include in decisions that affect them
- Celebrate their unique contributions

### Stage 2: Learner Safety
*"I feel safe to learn and ask questions"*

**Behaviors:**
- Asking "stupid questions"
- Admitting "I don't know"
- Trying new things
- Making mistakes while learning
- Asking for help

**How to create:**
- Praise questions ("Great question!")
- Share your own learning journey
- "There are no stupid questions"
- Normalize not knowing everything
- Celebrate learning from mistakes

### Stage 3: Contributor Safety
*"I feel safe to contribute my ideas and work"*

**Behaviors:**
- Sharing ideas
- Making suggestions
- Taking initiative
- Proposing changes
- Volunteering for work

**How to create:**
- Thank people for ideas (even if not used)
- "Yes, and..." instead of "Yes, but..."
- Give credit generously
- Encourage experimentation
- Implement team suggestions

### Stage 4: Challenger Safety
*"I feel safe to challenge the status quo"*

**Behaviors:**
- Questioning decisions
- Raising concerns
- Disagreeing with leadership
- Pointing out problems
- Suggesting big changes

**How to create:**
- Explicitly invite dissent
- Thank people who disagree
- Act on feedback
- Protect dissenters
- Reward constructive challenge

**Most teams struggle with Stage 4** - It requires the most trust.

## Signs of Psychological Safety

### High Psychological Safety

✓ People admit mistakes openly
✓ Healthy debate in meetings
✓ Junior people speak up
✓ Questions asked freely
✓ Concerns raised early
✓ Humor and laughter
✓ People help each other
✓ Conflict is productive
✓ Experimentation encouraged
✓ Failures discussed and learned from

### Low Psychological Safety

✗ Silence in meetings
✗ Only senior people talk
✗ Mistakes hidden
✗ People check out
✗ Backstabbing and politics
✗ Fear of asking questions
✗ No constructive disagreement
✗ Passive-aggressive behavior
✗ People leave the team
✗ Innovation dies

## Building Psychological Safety as a Manager

### 1. Model Vulnerability

**Admit your mistakes:**
"I made a mistake on the architecture decision. Here's what happened and what I learned."

**Share your learning:**
"I don't know Rust well. I'm learning it alongside you."

**Ask for help:**
"I'm stuck on this problem. Can someone help me think through it?"

**Show uncertainty:**
"I don't have all the answers. What do you all think?"

**Your vulnerability gives permission for theirs.**

### 2. Respond Well to Mistakes

**Bad response:**
- "How could you let this happen?"
- "This is unacceptable"
- Public shaming
- Punishment without learning

**Good response:**
- "What happened?"
- "What did we learn?"
- "How do we prevent this?"
- "Thanks for catching and fixing it"

**Script for mistakes:**
"Let's do a blameless post-mortem. What happened? What can we learn? How do we prevent it? No punishment - we want to learn, not point fingers."

### 3. Encourage Questions

**Phrases to use:**
- "Great question"
- "I'm glad you asked that"
- "That's an important thing to clarify"
- "Anyone else wondering the same thing?"

**When you don't know:**
"I don't know. Let's figure it out together."
"Great question. I'll find out and get back to you."

**Make it safe to not know:**
"What questions do you have? Remember, there are no stupid questions."

### 4. Invite Dissent

**Explicitly ask:**
- "What am I missing?"
- "What could go wrong with this?"
- "Who disagrees and why?"
- "What's the counterargument?"
- "Poke holes in this idea"

**When someone disagrees:**
- Thank them
- Probe deeper
- Take it seriously
- Don't get defensive

**Script:**
"Thanks for pushing back on this. Help me understand your concerns. What would you do differently?"

### 5. React to Bad News Well

**Your reaction to bad news determines if you'll hear it again.**

**Bad reactions:**
- Shooting the messenger
- Getting angry
- Blaming
- Minimizing

**Good reactions:**
- "Thank you for telling me"
- "I appreciate you bringing this up"
- Stay calm
- Ask questions to understand
- Focus on solving, not blaming

**Example:**
Team: "The release is going to be delayed a week"
You: "OK. Help me understand what happened and what we need to do. Thanks for telling me early so we can adjust."

### 6. Normalize Failure

**Celebrate intelligent failures:**
- Tried something new
- Learned from it
- Documented the learning
- Shared with team

**Example:**
"The experiment with [new framework] didn't work, but we learned [lessons] and that saved us from investing months in the wrong direction. Good hypothesis testing."

**Create "failure of the month" awards**
- Light-hearted recognition
- Normalizes mistakes
- Encourages risk-taking

### 7. Distribute Airtime

**Ensure everyone speaks:**
- Call on quiet people
- "Sarah, we haven't heard from you. Thoughts?"
- Round-robin formats
- Alternate async and sync input

**Stop dominators:**
- "Thanks Alex. Let's hear from others."
- "We've heard from [names]. Who else?"
- Pre-meeting: "Let others go first this time"

**Goal:** Everyone contributes, not just loudest voices.

### 8. Show Appreciation

**Recognize contributions:**
- Thank people for ideas (even rejected ones)
- Credit people's work
- Celebrate small wins
- Public praise, private criticism

**Make people feel valued:**
- Remember personal details
- Ask about their interests
- Celebrate milestones
- Show you care about them as humans

### 9. Be Consistent and Predictable

**Consistency builds trust:**
- Same standards for everyone
- Predictable reactions
- Follow through on commitments
- No surprises or gotchas

**Avoid:**
- Mood swings
- Playing favorites
- Moving goalposts
- Random outbursts

### 10. Address Violations

**When someone undermines safety:**
- Address immediately
- Private conversation
- Clear about impact
- Set expectations

**Example behaviors to address:**
- Mockery or ridicule
- Interrupting or dismissing
- Taking credit for others' work
- Retribution for speaking up
- Gossiping or backstabbing

**Script:**
"When you rolled your eyes at Jamie's question, it made them uncomfortable. Questions are welcome on this team. That reaction isn't OK here."

## Team Practices for Psychological Safety

### Blameless Post-Mortems

**After incidents:**
- Focus on systems, not people
- What happened (timeline)
- Why it happened (root causes)
- How to prevent (action items)
- No blame or punishment

See [Incident Management](./incident-management.md)

### Retrospectives

**Regular team reflection:**
- What went well?
- What could be better?
- What will we try?
- Safe space to raise concerns

See [Retrospectives](./retrospectives.md)

### Team Agreements

**Co-create norms:**
```
How We Work Together:

1. Assume positive intent
2. Ask questions freely
3. Admit mistakes openly
4. Disagree respectfully
5. Help each other
6. Protect each other's time
7. Have fun
```

**Review quarterly:** Are we living these?

### Regular Check-ins

**Pulse checks:**
- "How's everyone doing? Thumbs up/down/middle"
- "Energy level check: 1-5"
- "What's on your mind?"

**Temperature reads:**
- Start meetings with quick share
- Helps people surface concerns
- Builds connection

### Pair Programming

**Benefits for safety:**
- Learn together
- Share knowledge
- Build trust
- Normalize asking questions
- Collective code ownership

### Show and Tell

**Regular demos:**
- Everyone shows their work
- No judgment, just learning
- Celebrate progress
- Get feedback
- Build confidence

## Measuring Psychological Safety

### Survey Questions

**Rate 1-5 (Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree):**

1. If I make a mistake on this team, it is not held against me
2. Members of this team are able to bring up problems and tough issues
3. People on this team sometimes reject others for being different (reverse scored)
4. It is safe to take a risk on this team
5. It is easy to ask other members of this team for help
6. No one on this team would deliberately act in a way that undermines my efforts
7. Working with members of this team, my unique skills and talents are valued and utilized

**Interpretation:**
- Average >4: High psychological safety
- Average 3-4: Moderate, room for improvement
- Average <3: Low, needs urgent attention

**Use Amy Edmondson's validated scale for accuracy**

### Behavioral Indicators

**Track:**
- Number of questions asked in meetings
- Distribution of who speaks
- Frequency of dissenting opinions
- How mistakes are discussed
- Idea generation rate
- Participation in retrospectives

### Qualitative Signals

**In 1:1s, ask:**
- "Do you feel comfortable speaking up in team meetings?"
- "Can you disagree with me or others?"
- "Do you feel you can be yourself here?"
- "What makes you hesitant to share?"

## Threats to Psychological Safety

### Toxic Behaviors

**Immediately address:**
- Bullying or aggression
- Humiliation or mockery
- Stealing credit
- Retaliation for speaking up
- Exclusion or cliques
- Microaggressions

**Zero tolerance for:**
- Harassment
- Discrimination
- Threats
- Abusive behavior

### Structural Issues

**Undermine safety:**
- Unclear expectations
- Constantly changing priorities
- Lack of resources
- Unrealistic deadlines
- No support for learning
- Punishing failure

**Fix the structure:**
- Clear goals and priorities
- Adequate resources
- Reasonable timelines
- Training and support
- Celebrating learning

### Leadership Anti-Patterns

**Behaviors that kill safety:**
- Shooting the messenger
- Public criticism
- Favoritism
- Micromanagement
- Taking credit
- Unpredictability
- Breaking promises

**Fix yourself first:**
- Get feedback on your impact
- Work with coach if needed
- Commit to change
- Team observes and adjusts to you

## Rebuilding After Damage

### If Safety Has Been Broken

**Acknowledge it:**
"I know trust has been damaged. I want to rebuild it. Here's how..."

**Steps to rebuild:**

1. **Acknowledge the problem**
   - Name what happened
   - Take responsibility (if you contributed)
   - Express commitment to change

2. **Listen to the team**
   - What happened from their perspective?
   - How are people feeling?
   - What would rebuild trust?

3. **Make concrete changes**
   - Address root causes
   - Change behaviors/structures
   - Follow through on commitments

4. **Be patient**
   - Trust takes time to rebuild
   - Prove change through consistency
   - Don't expect quick forgiveness

5. **Check progress**
   - Regular pulse checks
   - Are things improving?
   - What else is needed?

**Rebuilding takes 3x longer than breaking.**

## Psychological Safety for Remote Teams

### Additional Challenges

**Harder to:**
- Read body language
- Have spontaneous conversations
- Build personal connections
- Include quieter people
- Notice when someone's struggling

### Remote-Specific Practices

**Over-communicate:**
- More frequent check-ins
- Explicit inclusion
- Video on for connection
- Async input options

**Create informal spaces:**
- Virtual coffee chats
- Slack social channels
- Virtual team activities
- Watercooler channels

**Equalize participation:**
- Chat for questions/comments
- Round-robin in video calls
- Anonymous input tools
- Async brainstorming

## Psychological Safety FAQs

### "Isn't this just being nice?"

No. Psychological safety includes:
- Direct feedback
- High standards
- Productive conflict
- Accountability

It's candor with kindness, not avoiding hard truths.

### "Won't people slack off?"

Evidence shows opposite:
- Higher performance
- More accountability
- Self-motivated improvement
- Peer support for excellence

### "How long does it take to build?"

- Initial signs: 2-3 months
- Solid foundation: 6-12 months
- Deep trust: 1-2 years
- Ongoing maintenance: Forever

### "What if someone abuses it?"

Address directly:
- Name the behavior
- Explain impact on team
- Set clear expectations
- Consequences if continues

Safety doesn't mean no accountability.

### "My manager undermines it. What can I do?"

**Limited options:**
- Talk to your manager (if safe)
- Create it within your team
- Escalate to HR if serious
- Document and protect yourself
- May need to leave (unfortunate reality)
