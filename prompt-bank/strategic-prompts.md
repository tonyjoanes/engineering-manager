# Strategic & Planning Prompts

Ready-to-use prompts for planning, prioritization, stakeholder management, and strategic decisions.

---

## Prioritization Decision

**When to Use:** You have multiple competing priorities and need to decide what to focus on.

**Related Resources:**
- [Planning & Prioritization](../practices/planning-prioritization.md)
- [Saying No](../heuristics/saying-no.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I need to prioritize between multiple competing initiatives/requests.

The options:
1. [Initiative 1: description, who's requesting, urgency]
2. [Initiative 2: description, who's requesting, urgency]
3. [Initiative 3: description, who's requesting, urgency]
[Add more as needed]

Context:
- Team capacity: [current bandwidth]
- Strategic goals: [what we're trying to achieve this quarter/year]
- Dependencies: [any blockers or prerequisites]
- Stakeholder pressure: [who's pushing for what]

Help me prioritize using frameworks from the planning guide:
1. Apply RICE scoring (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort)
2. Use value/effort matrix
3. Align with strategic goals
4. Consider opportunity cost
5. Make recommendation with rationale

Reference the planning & prioritization guide for frameworks.
```

**Expected Output:**
- RICE scores for each option
- Value/effort matrix positioning
- Strategic alignment assessment
- Prioritization recommendation
- Rationale for stakeholders

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Communicate decision with rationale
- Say no to deprioritized items (use saying no guide)
- Commit team to top priority
- Revisit in planning cycles

## Saying No to Request

**When to Use:** You need to decline a request from stakeholder, leadership, or team member.

**Related Resources:**
- [Saying No](../heuristics/saying-no.md)
- [Stakeholder Management](../leadership/stakeholder-management.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I need to say no to [specific request] from [person/team].

Request details:
- What they're asking for: [describe request]
- Why they want it: [their rationale]
- Your relationship with requestor: [peer / leadership / team member / external]
- Why you need to say no: [capacity / priorities / not the right approach / etc.]
- Risks of saying no: [political / relationship / business impact]
- Alternatives you could offer: [if any]

Help me craft a "positive no":
1. Acknowledge the request and its importance
2. Explain the no with clear rationale
3. Offer alternatives if possible
4. Maintain the relationship
5. Be firm but respectful

Reference the saying no guide for scripts for different scenarios.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Positive no script tailored to relationship
- Clear rationale
- Alternative offerings if available
- Relationship preservation strategy

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Deliver the no (in person or writing as appropriate)
- Document decision
- Follow through on alternatives if offered
- Maintain relationship

## Stakeholder Alignment

**When to Use:** Multiple stakeholders have conflicting expectations or priorities.

**Related Resources:**
- [Stakeholder Management](../leadership/stakeholder-management.md)
- [Planning & Prioritization](../practices/planning-prioritization.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I have conflicting expectations from multiple stakeholders.

Stakeholders and their positions:
- [Stakeholder 1]: wants [X], rationale: [why]
- [Stakeholder 2]: wants [Y], rationale: [why]
- [Stakeholder 3]: wants [Z], rationale: [why]

Context:
- Your team's capacity: [limited / somewhat flexible / etc.]
- Strategic priorities: [what leadership says is important]
- Timeline pressures: [any deadlines]
- Political dynamics: [who has influence, relationships]

Help me create alignment:
1. Understand each stakeholder's underlying needs (not just positions)
2. Identify areas of alignment vs true conflict
3. Develop options that address multiple needs
4. Create alignment strategy (meetings, communication plan)
5. Navigate political dynamics

Reference the stakeholder management guide for managing across.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Stakeholder needs analysis (underlying vs stated)
- Areas of alignment and conflict
- Options that balance needs
- Alignment meeting strategy
- Communication plan

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Schedule alignment meetings
- Facilitate discussion
- Drive to decision
- Communicate outcome clearly
- Manage expectations going forward

## Managing Up

**When to Use:** You need to manage your manager's expectations or get support from leadership.

**Related Resources:**
- [Stakeholder Management](../leadership/stakeholder-management.md)
- [Saying No](../heuristics/saying-no.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I need to manage up regarding [specific situation].

Situation:
- What you need: [support / decision / resources / air cover / pushback on request]
- Your manager's context: [their pressures, priorities, style]
- Current expectation mismatch: [what they expect vs what's realistic]
- Risks: [what happens if you don't address this]
- Supporting data: [metrics, feedback, examples you can share]

Help me prepare to manage up:
1. Frame the situation in terms of business impact
2. Come with data and recommendations, not just problems
3. Understand what decision you need from them
4. Anticipate their concerns and objections
5. Make it easy for them to support you

Reference the stakeholder management guide for managing up strategies.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Business impact framing
- Data-backed narrative
- Clear ask/recommendation
- Anticipated objections with responses
- Communication approach

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Schedule meeting with manager
- Present situation and recommendations
- Get decision or support
- Follow up on commitments
- Keep them informed

## Time Management Crisis

**When to Use:** You're overwhelmed with meetings, requests, and not enough time for strategic work.

**Related Resources:**
- [Time Management](../heuristics/time-management.md)
- [Saying No](../heuristics/saying-no.md)
- [Delegation](../leadership/delegation.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I'm overwhelmed and not spending time on what matters most.

Current situation:
- Meeting load: [hours/week in meetings]
- Types of meetings: [1:1s / status updates / stakeholder syncs / etc.]
- Time for strategic work: [almost none / occasional / etc.]
- Requests and interruptions: [constant / frequent / manageable]
- Delegation: [what you've delegated / what you're holding onto]
- Current priorities: [what you should be focusing on]

Help me reclaim my time:
1. Audit current time use (maker vs manager time)
2. Identify meetings to decline, delegate, or shorten
3. Create focus time blocks for strategic work
4. Set boundaries on interruptions
5. Delegate more effectively
6. Say no to non-essential requests

Reference time management, saying no, and delegation guides.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Time audit and analysis
- Meetings to cut/delegate/shorten
- Focus time blocking strategy
- Boundary-setting tactics
- Delegation opportunities
- Saying no scripts

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Decline or delegate meetings
- Block focus time on calendar
- Communicate boundaries
- Delegate identified tasks
- Protect strategic time ruthlessly

## Cross-Functional Initiative Planning

**When to Use:** Planning a project or initiative that requires multiple teams or functions.

**Related Resources:**
- [Planning & Prioritization](../practices/planning-prioritization.md)
- [Stakeholder Management](../leadership/stakeholder-management.md)
- [Team Topologies](../practices/team-topologies.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I'm planning [initiative name] that requires collaboration across [teams/functions involved].

Initiative details:
- Goal: [what we're trying to achieve]
- Teams involved: [Engineering, Product, Design, Sales, etc.]
- Timeline: [deadline or target]
- Dependencies: [between teams or on external factors]
- Risks: [technical, organizational, timeline]
- Success metrics: [how we'll measure success]

Help me create a plan:
1. Identify value stream and team boundaries (Team Topologies)
2. Map dependencies and plan collaboration mode (X-as-a-Service vs Collaboration)
3. Create project structure and governance
4. Identify risks and mitigation strategies
5. Set up communication and alignment cadence
6. Define success metrics and milestones

Reference planning & prioritization, stakeholder management, and Team Topologies guides.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Team collaboration model (Team Topologies)
- Dependency map with mitigation
- Project plan with milestones
- Communication cadence
- Risk register
- Success metrics

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Kick off with all teams
- Set up coordination meetings
- Track dependencies
- Monitor progress
- Communicate status regularly

## Roadmap Planning

**When to Use:** Creating or updating your team's technical or product roadmap.

**Related Resources:**
- [Planning & Prioritization](../practices/planning-prioritization.md)
- [Platform Engineering & DevEx](../practices/platform-engineering-devex.md)
- [Stakeholder Management](../leadership/stakeholder-management.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I need to create/update our [quarterly / annual] roadmap for [team name].

Input:
- Strategic goals: [company/org priorities]
- Stakeholder requests: [what different groups are asking for]
- Technical debt: [known issues, architectural improvements needed]
- Team capacity: [number of engineers, velocity]
- Current commitments: [ongoing work]
- Innovation budget: [time for exploration vs delivery]

Help me build a roadmap:
1. Categorize items (features, tech debt, platform, innovation)
2. Prioritize using value/effort/strategic alignment
3. Balance delivery vs investment (platform, tech debt)
4. Sequence work accounting for dependencies
5. Set realistic milestones with buffer
6. Communicate roadmap to stakeholders

Reference planning & prioritization guide for frameworks and platform engineering for balancing platform work.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Categorized and prioritized backlog
- Roadmap with quarterly/monthly milestones
- Balance of feature work, tech debt, platform, innovation
- Capacity plan
- Stakeholder communication approach

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Review with leadership
- Share with stakeholders
- Get team buy-in
- Track progress
- Adjust based on reality

## Technical Debt Prioritization

**When to Use:** Deciding how much time to spend on tech debt vs new features.

**Related Resources:**
- [Planning & Prioritization](../practices/planning-prioritization.md)
- [Stakeholder Management](../leadership/stakeholder-management.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I need to advocate for technical debt work.

Situation:
- Tech debt items: [list major tech debt items]
- Impact of debt: [slow development / incidents / quality issues / developer frustration]
- Current time on tech debt: [percentage or hours]
- Pressure for features: [stakeholder expectations]
- Team morale: [developer frustration level]
- Business impact: [how debt affects customers or business]

Help me:
1. Quantify impact of tech debt in business terms
2. Prioritize which debt to address first (highest impact)
3. Determine right balance (e.g., 20% tech debt, 80% features)
4. Create business case for stakeholders
5. Plan how to integrate tech debt into roadmap
6. Communicate value to non-technical stakeholders

Reference planning & prioritization and stakeholder management guides.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Tech debt impact quantified (time, money, risk)
- Prioritized tech debt list
- Recommended time allocation
- Business case with ROI
- Roadmap integration approach
- Stakeholder communication strategy

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Present business case to stakeholders
- Get agreement on allocation
- Add to roadmap
- Track velocity improvements
- Measure impact

## OKR/Goal Setting

**When to Use:** Setting quarterly or annual goals and objectives for your team.

**Related Resources:**
- [Planning & Prioritization](../practices/planning-prioritization.md)
- [Team Health Metrics](../practices/team-health-metrics.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I need to set [quarterly / annual] OKRs/goals for [team name].

Context:
- Company objectives: [top-level company OKRs]
- Team mission: [value stream or product area]
- Recent performance: [DORA metrics, achievements, challenges]
- Stakeholder expectations: [what different groups need]
- Team capacity and skills: [realistically what we can achieve]
- Growth areas: [where team needs to improve]

Help me create OKRs:
1. Align team objectives with company objectives
2. Make objectives ambitious but achievable
3. Define measurable key results (not tasks)
4. Balance outcome-focused with growth/improvement goals
5. Ensure objectives are meaningful to team motivation
6. Set success criteria (70% = good, 100% = exceptional)

Reference planning & prioritization guide for goal-setting frameworks.
```

**Expected Output:**
- 3-5 Objectives aligned to company goals
- 3-4 Key Results per objective (measurable)
- Success criteria for each KR
- Balance of delivery, quality, and growth
- Team buy-in strategy

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Review with team for buy-in
- Share with stakeholders
- Track progress weekly/monthly
- Adjust as needed
- Celebrate achievement

## Scope Creep Management

**When to Use:** A project or initiative is expanding beyond original scope.

**Related Resources:**
- [Saying No](../heuristics/saying-no.md)
- [Stakeholder Management](../leadership/stakeholder-management.md)
- [Planning & Prioritization](../practices/planning-prioritization.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
[Project name] is experiencing scope creep.

Original scope:
- Original goals: [what we agreed to build]
- Original timeline: [deadline]
- Original resources: [team size, time allocation]

Current situation:
- New requests: [what's being added]
- Who's requesting: [stakeholders adding scope]
- Timeline impact: [how this affects delivery]
- Team stress level: [are they overwhelmed]
- Risk: [what happens if we don't address this]

Help me manage scope creep:
1. Document original vs current scope clearly
2. Quantify impact on timeline and resources
3. Create decision framework (accept/defer/reject new requests)
4. Communicate trade-offs to stakeholders
5. Reset expectations and get alignment
6. Implement change control process

Reference saying no and stakeholder management guides.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Clear scope comparison (original vs current)
- Timeline and resource impact analysis
- Decision framework for new requests
- Stakeholder communication plan
- Change control process

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Meet with stakeholders to reset scope
- Implement change control
- Say no to non-essential additions
- Communicate trade-offs clearly
- Protect team from overload

## Competing Deadlines

**When to Use:** Multiple high-priority items all have urgent deadlines.

**Related Resources:**
- [Planning & Prioritization](../practices/planning-prioritization.md)
- [Stakeholder Management](../leadership/stakeholder-management.md)
- [Time Management](../heuristics/time-management.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I have multiple competing deadlines that can't all be met.

The commitments:
1. [Item 1: what, who needs it, deadline]
2. [Item 2: what, who needs it, deadline]
3. [Item 3: what, who needs it, deadline]

Context:
- Team capacity: [realistic bandwidth]
- Flexibility: [which deadlines are immovable vs negotiable]
- Consequences: [what happens if each slips]
- Stakeholder power: [who has most influence]
- Strategic importance: [which aligns most with goals]

Help me navigate this:
1. Assess true urgency vs stated urgency
2. Identify negotiable vs non-negotiable deadlines
3. Determine if sequencing or parallel work is possible
4. Decide what to commit to and what to renegotiate
5. Communicate decisions and trade-offs to stakeholders

Reference planning & prioritization and stakeholder management guides.
```

**Expected Output:**
- True priority assessment
- Deadline negotiability analysis
- Recommended sequencing or approach
- Stakeholder communication strategy
- Realistic commitments

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Communicate trade-offs to stakeholders
- Renegotiate timelines where possible
- Commit to achievable plan
- Execute with focus
- Update stakeholders regularly

## Building Influence Without Authority

**When to Use:** You need to influence a decision or outcome but don't have direct authority.

**Related Resources:**
- [Stakeholder Management](../leadership/stakeholder-management.md)
- [Difficult Conversations](../leadership/difficult-conversations.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I need to influence [specific decision or outcome] but don't have direct authority.

Situation:
- What you want to influence: [describe the decision or change]
- Who has decision authority: [name/role]
- Your relationship with them: [strong / neutral / weak]
- Why you care: [impact on your team, product, customers]
- Resistance or competing interests: [what you're up against]
- Your leverage: [expertise, data, relationships, etc.]

Help me build influence:
1. Understand decision-maker's priorities and concerns
2. Frame your position in terms of their goals
3. Build coalition of support if needed
4. Use data and evidence effectively
5. Timing and approach for maximum influence
6. Build long-term relationship for future influence

Reference stakeholder management guide for influence strategies.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Decision-maker analysis (priorities, concerns)
- Influence strategy tailored to their goals
- Supporting data and narrative
- Coalition-building approach if needed
- Timing and communication plan

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Build relationships with decision-maker
- Gather supporting data
- Rally support if appropriate
- Present case compellingly
- Follow up persistently but respectfully
