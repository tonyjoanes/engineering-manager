# One-on-One Prompts

Ready-to-use prompts for different one-on-one scenarios and conversations.

---

## Standard 1:1 Preparation

**When to Use:** Preparing for a regular weekly or bi-weekly 1:1.

**Related Resources:**
- [One-on-Ones](../leadership/one-on-ones.md)
- [GROW Model](../leadership/grow-model.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I have a 1:1 coming up with [team member name].

Context:
- Relationship: [new / established / strong / needs work]
- Last 1:1 takeaways: [what we discussed, action items]
- Recent observations: [their recent work, mood, performance]
- Current projects: [what they're working on]
- Any concerns: [performance, engagement, burnout, etc.]
- Their agenda items: [if they've shared any]

Help me prepare:
1. Review progress on previous action items
2. Identify topics to discuss (using 5-15-7-3 structure from 1:1 guide)
3. Questions to ask about their work, growth, and well-being
4. Coaching opportunities if any
5. Follow-up actions to document

Reference the one-on-ones guide for the 30-minute structure and question banks.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Structured agenda using 5-15-7-3 framework
- Specific questions to ask
- Topics to cover
- Coaching approach if needed
- Action items to track

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Conduct 1:1
- Document key points
- Track action items
- Follow up on commitments

## Career Conversation 1:1

**When to Use:** Team member wants to discuss career growth, promotion, or long-term goals.

**Related Resources:**
- [One-on-Ones](../leadership/one-on-ones.md)
- [GROW Model](../leadership/grow-model.md)
- [Career Development](../leadership/career-development.md)
- [DevOps Career Ladder](../practices/devops-career-ladder.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
[Team member name] wants to discuss career growth in our next 1:1.

Background:
- Current role and level: [title, level]
- Time in role: [duration]
- Performance: [exceeds / meets / developing]
- Career interests: [what they've mentioned before]
- Strengths: [list]
- Growth areas: [list]
- Specific ask: [promotion / new role / skills development / etc.]

Using the GROW model, help me structure a career conversation:
1. Goal: Explore their career aspirations and definition of success
2. Reality: Assess current skills, performance, and readiness
3. Options: Identify development paths and opportunities
4. Way Forward: Create concrete development plan with milestones

Also reference the relevant career ladder if applicable (DevOps or general engineering).
```

**Expected Output:**
- GROW-based conversation structure
- Questions to explore their goals
- Gap analysis (current vs target)
- Development plan framework
- Timeline and milestones

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Document development plan
- Identify stretch assignments
- Schedule regular progress check-ins
- Track skill development

## Performance Concern 1:1

**When to Use:** You need to address a performance issue in a 1:1.

**Related Resources:**
- [One-on-Ones](../leadership/one-on-ones.md)
- [Feedback Models](../leadership/feedback-models.md)
- [Underperformance](../leadership/underperformance.md)
- [Difficult Conversations](../leadership/difficult-conversations.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I need to address a performance concern with [team member name] in our 1:1.

Issue details:
- Specific concern: [describe the performance issue]
- Examples: [specific instances with dates]
- Impact: [on team, project, customers]
- Previous discussions: [none / informal / formal]
- Possible causes: [skill gap / motivation / external factors / etc.]

Help me prepare:
1. Structure the conversation using SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact)
2. Open with care and directness
3. Invite their perspective
4. Explore root causes
5. Co-create improvement plan
6. Set clear expectations and timeline

Reference the feedback models, underperformance, and difficult conversations guides.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Conversation structure with opening statement
- SBI-formatted feedback
- Questions to understand root cause
- Improvement plan framework
- Clear expectations and timeline

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Have the conversation
- Document discussion and plan
- Set check-in cadence
- Monitor progress
- Escalate if no improvement

## Burnout Check-In 1:1

**When to Use:** You notice signs of burnout or stress in a team member.

**Related Resources:**
- [One-on-Ones](../leadership/one-on-ones.md)
- [Burnout Prevention](../heuristics/burnout-prevention.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I'm concerned about potential burnout with [team member name].

Observable signs:
- [specific behaviors: withdrawal, irritability, missed deadlines, etc.]
- Duration: [how long you've noticed this]
- Recent context: [big project, long hours, personal issues if known, etc.]
- Their normal state: [usually energetic / always stressed / etc.]

Help me structure a supportive 1:1:
1. Open with care and observation (not accusation)
2. Ask about their experience and well-being
3. Explore stressors (work-related and life-related if they share)
4. Identify immediate relief actions
5. Create recovery and prevention plan
6. Show support without being invasive

Reference the burnout prevention guide for signs and interventions.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Empathetic opening statement
- Open-ended questions about well-being
- Active listening approach
- Immediate relief options (time off, workload adjustment)
- Longer-term prevention strategy

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Adjust workload immediately
- Grant time off if needed
- Address systemic stressors
- Regular well-being check-ins
- Monitor recovery

## Skip-Level 1:1

**When to Use:** Having a 1:1 with someone who reports to your direct report.

**Related Resources:**
- [One-on-Ones](../leadership/one-on-ones.md)
- [Stakeholder Management](../leadership/stakeholder-management.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I'm having a skip-level 1:1 with [skip-level team member name] who reports to [their manager name].

Context:
- Purpose: [regular skip-level / specific concern / get to know them / etc.]
- Their role: [title and level]
- Relationship with their manager: [strong / developing / issues if known]
- Team/org context: [any relevant background]
- Specific goals for this 1:1: [build connection / surface issues / etc.]

Help me structure a productive skip-level 1:1:
1. Set appropriate expectations (not going around their manager)
2. Questions to understand their experience
3. How to surface concerns without undermining their manager
4. What to ask about their growth and satisfaction
5. How to follow up on what I learn

Reference the one-on-ones guide for question banks and stakeholder management for managing relationships.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Opening statement about purpose
- Questions about their experience, growth, challenges
- Approach to handle any concerns about their manager
- How to close and follow up
- What to share back with their manager

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Thank them for their time
- Share relevant feedback with their manager
- Address any systemic issues
- Build relationship over time

## New Team Member First 1:1

**When to Use:** First one-on-one with a new team member.

**Related Resources:**
- [One-on-Ones](../leadership/one-on-ones.md)
- [Onboarding](../leadership/onboarding.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
I'm having my first 1:1 with [new team member name] who just joined as [role].

Background:
- Start date: [when they started]
- How long they've been here: [days/weeks]
- Previous experience: [background if known]
- Onboarding progress: [technical setup, initial tasks, etc.]
- Early impressions: [if you have any]

Help me structure a great first 1:1:
1. Build rapport and connection
2. Understand their background, working style, and motivations
3. Set expectations for our 1:1s going forward
4. Learn how they like to receive feedback
5. Understand their career goals
6. Check on onboarding experience
7. Make them feel welcomed and supported

Reference the one-on-ones and onboarding guides.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Conversation structure for first 1:1
- Getting-to-know-you questions
- Working style questions
- 1:1 format expectations
- Onboarding check-in
- Welcome and support message

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Set up regular 1:1 cadence
- Document their preferences and goals
- Address any onboarding gaps
- Connect them with team

```

Context:

- Consistent strengths: [what they excel at]
- Career trajectory: [on track for promotion / recently promoted / etc.]
- Retention risk: [low / medium / high]
- Growth interests: [what they've expressed interest in]

Help me structure a recognition and retention 1:1:

2. Understand what motivates them and keeps them engaged
3. Explore their career goals and ensure we're supporting them
4. Identify new challenges or stretch opportunities
```

- Specific recognition script with examples

- Career conversation approach
- Stretch opportunity ideas
- Retention strategy

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Deliver recognition
- Provide stretch assignments
- Advocate for their promotion if ready

## 1:1 with Struggling Team Member

**When to Use:** Team member is struggling technically or professionally and needs support.

**Related Resources:**
- [One-on-Ones](../leadership/one-on-ones.md)
- [GROW Model](../leadership/grow-model.md)
- [Underperformance](../leadership/underperformance.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
[Team member name] is struggling with [specific area: technical skills / time management / communication / etc.].

Situation:
- Specific struggles: [observable issues]
- Impact: [on their work, team, or projects]
- Possible causes: [skill gap / confidence / overwhelm / etc.]
- Their awareness: [they know / they don't realize / they're frustrated]
- Support provided so far: [what you've tried]

Using the GROW model, help me structure a supportive coaching 1:1:
1. Goal: What success looks like in this area
2. Reality: Current state and what's getting in the way
3. Options: Different approaches or support they could use
4. Way Forward: Specific actions and support I can provide

Keep it supportive and growth-oriented, not punitive.
```

**Expected Output:**
- GROW-based coaching conversation
- Root cause exploration
- Support and development options
- Action plan with your support
- Encouragement and belief in their ability

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Provide coaching or training
- Adjust workload if needed
- Pair them with mentor
- Frequent check-ins
- Celebrate small improvements

## Engagement Check 1:1

**When to Use:** Team member seems disengaged or their energy has changed.

**Related Resources:**
- [One-on-Ones](../leadership/one-on-ones.md)
- [Psychological Safety](../practices/psychological-safety.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
[Team member name] seems less engaged recently.

Observable changes:
- [specific behaviors: quiet in meetings, less initiative, minimal communication, etc.]
- Timeline: [when you started noticing this]
- Previous engagement level: [normally very engaged / always quiet / etc.]
- Recent events: [project ended, team changes, passed over for promotion, etc.]

Help me structure a 1:1 to understand and re-engage:
1. Open with genuine curiosity and care
2. Ask about their experience and satisfaction
3. Explore what might have changed
4. Understand what would increase their engagement
5. Identify actions I can take to help

Make it safe for them to be honest without fear of repercussions.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Empathetic opening
- Open-ended questions about their experience
- Active listening approach
- Exploration of factors affecting engagement
- Action plan to address concerns

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Address legitimate concerns
- Provide new challenges if bored
- Fix team or process issues
- Monitor engagement
- Regular check-ins

## 1:1 About Team Conflict

**When to Use:** Team member comes to you about conflict with a colleague.

**Related Resources:**
- [One-on-Ones](../leadership/one-on-ones.md)
- [Conflict Resolution](../leadership/conflict-resolution.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
[Team member name] wants to discuss conflict/tension with [other person].

What you know:
- Their description of conflict: [what they've said]
- Observable impacts: [on work, team dynamics]
- Other person's perspective: [if you know it / haven't heard yet]
- History: [first occurrence / ongoing tension]
- Type of conflict: [task / process / relationship / values if you can tell]

Help me structure the 1:1:
1. Listen fully to their perspective
2. Ask clarifying questions without taking sides
3. Explore their role and the other person's perspective
4. Discuss resolution approaches
5. Decide on next steps (coaching them / mediation / etc.)

Reference the conflict resolution guide for approaches.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Active listening approach
- Clarifying questions to understand fully
- Exploration of both perspectives
- Resolution options (direct conversation, mediation, coaching)
- Next steps with clear ownership

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Coach them on direct conversation if appropriate
- Hear other person's perspective
- Mediate if needed
- Follow up on resolution
- Address systemic issues

## Feedback Request 1:1

**When to Use:** Team member asks for feedback on their performance.

**Related Resources:**
- [One-on-Ones](../leadership/one-on-ones.md)
- [Feedback Models](../leadership/feedback-models.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
[Team member name] is asking for feedback on [specific area or general performance].

Background:
- What they're asking about: [specific project / general performance / skill area]
- Their current performance: [strong / solid / needs improvement]
- Specific examples: [recent work you can reference]
- Areas of strength: [list]
- Development opportunities: [list]
- Context for request: [performance review coming / want to improve / seeking promotion]

Help me structure feedback using SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact):
1. Acknowledge their request for feedback positively
2. Provide specific, balanced feedback (strengths + growth areas)
3. Use concrete examples
4. Make it actionable
5. Offer ongoing support

Reference the feedback models guide for SBI and other frameworks.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Structured feedback using SBI
- Specific examples for strengths and growth areas
- Actionable recommendations
- Development support offer
- Ongoing feedback commitment

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Document feedback themes
- Provide resources or support
- Regular feedback going forward
- Track improvement in growth areas

## Life Event Support 1:1

**When to Use:** Team member is going through a significant life event (positive or challenging).

**Related Resources:**
- [One-on-Ones](../leadership/one-on-ones.md)
- [Remote/Hybrid Teams](../leadership/remote-hybrid-teams.md)

**The Prompt:**
```
[Team member name] is going through [life event: new baby, illness, moving, family issue, etc.].

Context:
- The situation: [what's happening]
- Impact on work: [will they need flexibility / time off / reduced load]
- What they've shared: [how much they've told you]
- Your relationship: [strong / professional / new]
- Support available: [parental leave, flexible hours, EAP, etc.]

Help me structure a supportive 1:1:
1. Express appropriate support and empathy
2. Understand what they need (time, flexibility, reduced load)
3. Discuss options and company support available
4. Set realistic expectations for work during this time
5. Check in on their well-being
6. Plan for their transition or return

Be supportive while respecting boundaries.
```

**Expected Output:**
- Empathetic opening
- Questions about what support they need
- Options for flexibility, time off, workload
- Clear plan with realistic expectations
- Well-being focus

**Follow-Up Actions:**
- Arrange support (time off, flex, reduced load)
- Adjust team plans accordingly
- Regular check-ins (appropriate frequency)
- Ease back into work when ready
