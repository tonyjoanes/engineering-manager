# Time Management for Managers

Managing your time and energy effectively as an engineering manager.

## The Manager's Time Challenge

**Your time is not your own:**
- Meetings all day
- Interruptions
- Team needs
- Your manager's asks
- Cross-functional demands
- Escalations

**Yet you still need:**
- Strategic thinking time
- 1:1 prep
- Hiring and reviews
- Technical staying current
- Personal development

**The struggle is real.**

## Maker Time vs Manager Time

**Paul Graham's insight:**

**Maker Time (IC):**
- Long uninterrupted blocks
- Flow state
- Deep work
- 4+ hour chunks

**Manager Time:**
- Chopped up into meetings
- Context switching
- Responsive
- 30-min increments

**As manager:**
- Mostly manager time
- Some maker time needed
- Protect it fiercely

## Time Audit

### Where Does Time Go?

**Track for one week:**
- Meetings
- 1:1s
- Email/Slack
- Coding (if any)
- Hiring
- Admin
- Interruptions
- Strategic work

**Analysis:**
- What's essential?
- What's valuable?
- What's waste?
- What could delegate?
- What's missing?

### The Eisenhower Matrix

```
           Urgent
             ↑
   Q1       | Q2
   Crisis   | Strategy
   DO NOW   | PLAN
←──────────┼──────────→
   Q3       | Q4
   Distrac- | Time
   tions    | Wasters
   DELEGATE | ELIMINATE
             ↓
        Not Urgent
```

**Q1 - Urgent & Important:**
- Do immediately
- Minimize through planning

**Q2 - Not Urgent & Important:**
- Strategic work
- Planning
- Development
- Schedule time for this!

**Q3 - Urgent & Not Important:**
- Delegate
- Automate
- Say no

**Q4 - Not Urgent & Not Important:**
- Eliminate
- Just stop doing

## Calendar Management

### Block Your Calendar

**Protect time for:**
- Deep work (2-4 hour blocks)
- 1:1 prep
- Email/Slack catch-up
- Lunch
- Breaks
- Strategic thinking

**Example calendar:**
```
Monday:
9-10am: Email/Slack
10-11:30am: 1:1s
11:30-12pm: Prep
12-1pm: Lunch
1-3pm: Focus Block (no meetings)
3-5pm: Meetings OK

Friday:
9-12pm: No meetings (planning/reflection)
1-5pm: Meetings OK
```

### Meeting Hygiene

**Before accepting:**
- Is this necessary?
- Am I the right person?
- Can I send someone else?
- Can I read notes instead?

**For meetings you run:**
- Clear agenda
- Right attendees only
- Start/end on time
- Action items captured
- Follow up async

**Say no to:**
- No-agenda meetings
- Too many attendees
- Back-to-back marathons
- Could-be-an-email

### Buffer Time

**Don't book solid:**
- 5-10 min between meetings
- Bathroom, water, stretch
- Mental reset
- Unexpected delays

**Leave flex time:**
- 20% unscheduled
- For urgent issues
- For strategic work
- For breathing

## Email & Slack

### Inbox Zero (or Near-Zero)

**Process, don't revisit:**
- Read once
- Respond, delegate, delete, or file
- Don't leave as "to-do"

**Time-box email:**
- 9am: 30 min
- 12pm: 20 min
- 4pm: 30 min
- That's it

**Not during deep work:**
- Close email
- Close Slack
- Focus

### Slack Management

**Set boundaries:**
- Status: "In meeting" / "Focus time"
- Notifications off during focus
- Don't expect immediate responses

**Channels:**
- Leave unnecessary ones
- Mute low-priority
- Threads for discussions

**Async default:**
- Not everything is urgent
- Let people respond when available
- Document decisions

## Delegation

**Your superpower:**
- Delegate everything you can
- Frees time for what only you can do
- Develops your team

**What to delegate:**
- Operational tasks
- Repetitive work
- Growth opportunities
- Things others can do 70% as well

**Don't delegate:**
- Performance reviews
- Sensitive HR matters
- Your manager's asks
- Setting team vision

See [Delegation Framework](../leadership/delegation.md)

## Energy Management

### Energy > Time

**Not all hours equal:**
- Morning energy different from evening
- Post-lunch slump
- Meeting fatigue
- Context switch cost

**Match tasks to energy:**
- High energy: Strategic work, difficult decisions
- Medium energy: Meetings, 1:1s
- Low energy: Email, admin, reading

### Protect Your Energy

**What drains energy:**
- Back-to-back meetings
- Difficult conversations
- Context switching
- Solving others' problems
- Negativity

**What restores energy:**
- Breaks
- Exercise
- Nature
- Accomplishment
- Positive interactions
- Deep work

**Schedule accordingly:**
- Don't stack draining things
- Recharge between
- Mix energy types
- End day on positive if possible

## Focus Time

### Deep Work Blocks

**Schedule sacred time:**
- 2-4 hours
- No meetings
- No interruptions
- Door closed / DND

**Use for:**
- Strategic planning
- Writing (reviews, docs)
- Learning
- Thinking

**Protect fiercely:**
- Say no to meeting requests
- Reschedule only for emergencies
- Train team to respect it

### Minimizing Interruptions

**Set office hours:**
- "I'm available 2-3pm for questions"
- Reduces random interrupts
- People batch their questions

**Async by default:**
- Slack message, don't expect immediate response
- Document decisions
- Use email for non-urgent

**Emergency protocol:**
- Define what's an emergency
- How to reach you when urgent
- Otherwise, can wait

## Saying No

**You can't do everything:**
- Every yes is a no to something else
- Protect your priorities
- Decline gracefully

**How to say no:**
"I'd love to help, but I'm at capacity. Can we reprioritize, or can someone else take this?"

"That sounds interesting, but it doesn't align with our Q goals. Let's revisit next quarter."

"I can't commit to this right now. If X priority drops, I can pick this up."

See [Saying No Gracefully](./saying-no.md)

## Batching

### Group Similar Tasks

**Instead of:** Responding to emails all day
**Do:** 3 focused email sessions

**Batch:**
- Email
- Slack
- 1:1s (same day if possible)
- Code reviews
- Hiring interviews
- Admin tasks

**Benefits:**
- Less context switching
- More efficient
- Protected focus time

## Planning

### Weekly Planning

**Friday or Monday morning:**
- Review calendar
- Block focus time
- Identify priorities
- Schedule prep time
- Flag conflicts

**Questions:**
- What must get done?
- What can I delegate?
- What can wait?
- What should I cancel?

### Daily Planning

**Start of day (10 min):**
- Top 3 priorities
- Must-dos
- Quick wins
- Prep for meetings

**End of day (10 min):**
- What got done?
- What's carrying over?
- Tomorrow's priorities
- Celebrate wins

### Quarterly Planning

**Bigger picture:**
- Team goals
- Your development
- What to stop doing?
- What to start?
- Calendar audit

## Common Time Wasters

### Too Many Meetings

**Audit:**
- Which add value?
- Which could be email?
- Which could send delegate?
- Which could be shorter?

**Solutions:**
- Decline unnecessary
- 25/50 min defaults (not 30/60)
- Standing meetings → as-needed
- Async updates where possible

### Perfectionism

**Good enough is enough:**
- 80/20 rule
- Diminishing returns
- Perfect is enemy of done

**When to be perfect:**
- Public presentations
- Performance reviews
- Hiring decisions

**When good enough:**
- Internal emails
- Draft documents
- Slack messages
- Most things

### Email/Slack Rabbit Holes

**Set limits:**
- Timer for email sessions
- Close after time-box
- Don't refresh constantly
- Batch responses

### Unplanned Interruptions

**Office hours:**
- Set times for drop-ins
- Otherwise, Slack first

**Train your team:**
- Try solving first
- Batch non-urgent questions
- Respect focus time
- Escalation guidelines

## Boundary Setting

### Work Hours

**Define your hours:**
- Start and end time
- Communicate them
- Model healthy boundaries
- No guilt

**After hours:**
- Emergency only
- Don't check email
- Let voicemail get it
- It can wait

### Vacation

**Actually take it:**
- Unplug fully
- Delegate coverage
- Auto-responder
- Trust your team

**Prep for vacation:**
- Handoff document
- Coverage assigned
- Urgent items resolved
- Lower expectations

**Build systems:**
- Recurring calendar blocks
- Templates
- Checklists
- Automation

**Don't rely on:**
- Remembering
- Motivation
- Perfect discipline

## Self-Care

### You Can't Pour from Empty Cup

**Protect:**
- Sleep (7-8 hours)
- Exercise (3-4x week)
- Meals (don't skip)
- Breaks (throughout day)
- Social time
- Hobbies

**Signs you're burned out:**
- Always tired
- Irritable
- Dread work
- Health issues
- Relationships suffering

**Act early:**
- Take vacation
- Delegate more
- Say no more
- Get help

See [Avoiding Burnout](./burnout-prevention.md)

## Measuring Success

**You're managing time well if:**
- Accomplishing priorities
- Not always overwhelmed
- Have energy left
- Time for strategic work
- Team supported
- Work-life balance
- Sustainably paced

**Red flags:**
- Always behind
- No focus time
- Weekends/evenings
- Reactive, not proactive
- Neglecting health
- Relationships suffering
